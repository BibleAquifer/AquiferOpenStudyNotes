## Haggai 1:1 (id: 13047)

* This introductory statement (*superscription*) identifies the author, audience, date, and occasion for the prophecy.
* *In the second year*: This refers to the second year of the reign of Darius I, also called Darius the son of Hystaspes. He ruled Persia from 521 to 486 BC, early in the Persian Empire, from 539 to 331 BC. The messages of Haggai are among the most precisely dated prophecies in the Old Testament.
* The term *prophet* (Hebrew *nabi’*) identifies Haggai as a representative of God, speaking with God's authority.
* Zerubbabel led one group of Hebrews back to Judah after the Babylonian exile. At the time of Haggai’s ministry, he served as the Persian\-appointed governor of Judah (see [Ezra 2:1–2](https://ref.ly/Ezra2:1-Ezra2:2); [3:2](https://ref.ly/Ezra3:2); [Nehemiah 7:7](https://ref.ly/Neh7:7)).
* Jeshua was the high priest at that time. He supervised the rebuilding of the altar and the dedication of the second temple ([Ezra 3:2](https://ref.ly/Ezra3:2); [5:2](https://ref.ly/Ezra5:2); [6:15](https://ref.ly/Ezra6:15)).

## Haggai 1:2 (id: 13048)

* *LORD of Hosts*: Haggai and Zechariah often use this term for God to highlight the unstoppable power of God's word. The Lord can summon countless heavenly troops to fulfill his will instantly ([2 Kings 6:17](https://ref.ly/2Kgs6:17); [Matthew 26:53](https://ref.ly/Matt26:53)). This idea aimed to encourage the Judeans, who felt weak and unimportant.
* *The time has not yet come*: Poor crop yields from drought and pests had severely weakened Judah’s economy ([Haggai 1:6](https://ref.ly/Hag1:6), [9–11](https://ref.ly/Hag1:9-Hag1:11)), leading the people to believe they could not afford to rebuild the temple. Haggai convinced them that they could not afford to leave the temple in ruins, as God would not bless and prosper them if they did not rebuild the Lord’s house ([Haggai 1:4](https://ref.ly/Hag1:4), [7–8](https://ref.ly/Hag1:7-Hag1:8)).

* **Associated Passages:** Haggai 1:2

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`); House (ID: `realia:House`)

## Haggai 1:4 (id: 13049)

* *Is it a time for you*: Rhetorical questions in prophetic literature seek agreement, not a reply (see [Haggai 2:3](https://ref.ly/Hag2:3)). This question aims to prevent listeners from making excuses in response to the message.
* The people's luxurious (*paneled*) houses contrasted with God's temple, which was desolate (*in ruins*) and unusable.

* **Associated Passages:** Haggai 1:4

## Haggai 1:5 (id: 13050)

*Consider carefully your ways* (literally *Set your heart on these matters*): For the Hebrews, the *heart* is where thinking, feeling, and willing occur. This command (also [Haggai 1:7](https://ref.ly/Hag1:7); [2:15](https://ref.ly/Hag2:15), [18](https://ref.ly/Hag2:18)) urges people to think carefully and understand the link between their situation (drought and poverty, [Haggai 1:6](https://ref.ly/Hag1:6)) and their failure to restore proper worship of the Lord ([1:8–9](https://ref.ly/Hag1:8-Hag1:9)).

* *a bag pierced through*: This image represents the loss of wages. Many families faced poverty despite their steady work.

## Haggai 2:1 (id: 13064)

Haggai’s message aimed to give the people hope and encouragement during their current distress and discouragement.

## Haggai 2:5 (id: 13071)

* *My Spirit remains among you*: See[Exodus 29:46](https://ref.ly/Exod29:46); [Isaiah 63:11](https://ref.ly/Isa63:11); [Ezekiel 36:26–27](https://ref.ly/Ezek36:26-Ezek36:27).

## Haggai 2:6–7 (id: 13072)

God will once more shake the heavens and the earth on the coming day of judgment (see [Isaiah 2:19–21](https://ref.ly/Isa2:19-Isa2:21); [30:28](https://ref.ly/Isa30:28)). The previous shaking was the judgment on Egypt during the exodus ([Haggai 2:21–22](https://ref.ly/Hag2:21-Hag2:22); [Exodus 14:31](https://ref.ly/Exod14:31)).

Haggai likely saw God's ultimate judgment hinted at in events after his time, such as the fall of Persia to Greece and Greece to Rome (see [Daniel 2:39–45](https://ref.ly/Dan2:39-Dan2:45)).

## Haggai 2:9 (id: 13074)

* *latter glory*: Haggai might refer to the Messiah coming to his temple (see [Malachi 3:1](https://ref.ly/Mal3:1)). Jesus was presented in the Lord’s temple as a baby ([Luke 2:22](https://ref.ly/Luke2:22)), and he taught there as an adult ([Luke 19:45–47](https://ref.ly/Luke19:45-Luke19:47)). Jesus, the human Word of God, is greater than the temple ([Matthew 12:6](https://ref.ly/Matt12:6); see [John 2:13–22](https://ref.ly/John2:13-John2:22)). Although only a few recognized it, the presence of Jesus in the temple surpassed the glory in the tabernacle during the time of Moses and in Solomon’s Temple (compare [Luke 2:29–32](https://ref.ly/Luke2:29-Luke2:32)).
* *in this place I will provide peace*: The priestly blessing ([Numbers 6:24–26](https://ref.ly/Num6:24-Num6:26)) was part of the temple ceremonies. In the last days, God would make a covenant of peace with Israel ([Ezekiel 34:25](https://ref.ly/Ezek34:25); [37:26](https://ref.ly/Ezek37:26)). Rebuilding the temple guaranteed that future *peace*.

## Haggai 2:20–23 (id: 13065)

Haggai's final message is perhaps the most important. It focuses on the importance of David's descendant in Israel's religious and political life. After the Babylonian exile, the family line of David, in which kings came from the same family, was essential to Israel’s hope of restoration (see [Jeremiah 23:5](https://ref.ly/Jer23:5); [Ezekiel 37:24](https://ref.ly/Ezek37:24)). 

God had cursed David's descendant, King Jehoiachin, during the exile in Babylon ([Jeremiah 22:24–30](https://ref.ly/Jer22:24-Jer22:30)), but Haggai's last message lifts that curse. It restores the covenant with David (see [2 Samuel 7:4–17](https://ref.ly/2Sam7:4-2Sam7:17)) as the way God will fulfill his promises to bless and restore Israel.

* **Associated Passages:** Haggai 2:20-23

## Haggai 2:21 (id: 13066)

Zerubbabel, the governor, was a descendant of David through Jehoiachin ([1 Chronicles 3:19](https://ref.ly/1Chr3:19)). Haggai’s statement reverses the curse on Jehoiachin ([Jeremiah 22:24–30](https://ref.ly/Jer22:24-Jer22:30)). However, Zerubbabel suddenly disappears from the biblical record. He might have been removed as Judean governor or even executed by King Darius, who was trying to control his new empire. The expectations placed on Zerubbabel and his lineage as a descendant of David might have made him a political threat to Darius.

* **Associated Passages:** Haggai 2:21

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Tribe of Judah (ID: `group:Judah`); Judah (ID: `person:Judah.4`); Zerubbabel (ID: `person:Zerubbabel.2`)

## Haggai 2:22 (id: 13067)

*I will overturn chariots and their riders*: The prophet's words remind Israel of their escape from the Egyptian army (see [Exodus 15:1–21](https://ref.ly/Exod15:1-Exod15:21)). It is unclear if Haggai refers to future events or something closer, like the Persian Empire's conflicts. ## Haggai 2:23 (id: 13068)

The *signet ring* was a symbol of kingship. It was a finger ring made of gold or silver with an engraved stone. People used it to seal or approve official documents.

This image shows the divine authority that God gave to Zerubbabel. It assured God’s people that the Lord was still involved in political life, even after the failures of the kings of Judah.

Calling Zerubbabel the Lord’s *signet* likely renewed hope for the Messiah (God's chosen leader), since Zerubbabel was a descendant of King David. However, this promise ultimately points beyond Zerubbabel (see study note on Haggai 2:21). It points to one of his descendants, the Messiah, who would come later (see [Matthew 1:12](https://ref.ly/Matt1:12); [Luke 1:32–33](https://ref.ly/Luke1:32-Luke1:33); [3:27](https://ref.ly/Luke3:27)).
