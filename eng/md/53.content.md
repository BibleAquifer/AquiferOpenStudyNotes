## 2 Thessalonians 1:2 (id: 15204)

God our Father *and* the Lord Jesus Christ are the co\-equal source of grace and peace. The Thessalonians' hope relied on God's grace ([2:16](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:16)). Even during times when Christians were treated badly, they could feel peace from God ([3:16](https://ref.ly/2Thess3:16)).

## 2 Thessalonians 1:3 (id: 15206)

* *Brothers*: Greek *adelphoi*, a term for family members, both male and female.
* *thank God*: The apostle Paul starts by expressing thanks for the Thessalonians’ *faith* and *love*, and how they are *enduring* ([1:4](https://ref.ly/2Thess1:4)). These are key Christian virtues (see [1 Corinthians 13:13](https://ref.ly/1Cor13:13); [Colossians 1:4–5](https://ref.ly/Col1:4-Col1:5); [Hebrews 10:21–24](https://ref.ly/Heb10:21-Heb10:24); [1 Peter 2:1–22](https://ref.ly/1Pet2:1-1Pet2:22)). These virtues were increasing in the church despite the bad treatment they encountered ([2 Thessalonians 1:5](https://ref.ly/2Thess1:5)).

* **Associated Passages:** 2 Thessalonians 1:3

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Love (ID: `keyterm:Love`); Obligated (ID: `keyterm:Ought`); Believe (ID: `keyterm:Believe`); LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)

## 2 Thessalonians 2:4 (id: 15221)

* *exalt himself*: Like other Roman cities, Thessalonica built temples to worship the emperor (king). This religion (cult) was the model for the worship described here.
* *he will seat himself in the temple of God*: Antiochus Epiphanes polluted the temple in Jerusalem in 167 BC (see [Daniel 9:27](https://ref.ly/Dan9:27); [11:31](https://ref.ly/Dan11:31); [12:11](https://ref.ly/Dan12:11)). This might have anticipated the event prophesied here ([Matthew 24:15](https://ref.ly/Matt24:15); [Mark 13:14](https://ref.ly/Mark13:14)). It could also refer to Emperor Caligula's attempt to place his image in the temple in AD 40\.
* Alternatively, the temple might be a temple of the emperor. Here, *of God* could mean of *the* god. They will build this temple to honor the man of lawlessness. It is not necessarily the temple in Jerusalem.
* *proclaiming himself to be God* ([Ezekiel 28:2–10](https://ref.ly/Ezek28:2-Ezek28:10)): In the first century AD, people often gave divine titles to the emperor.

* **Associated Passages:** 2 Thessalonians 2:4

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`); Object of Worship (ID: `keyterm:ObjectOfWorship`); Temple (ID: `realia:Temple`)

## 2 Thessalonians 2:6 (id: 15222)

*what is now restraining him*: People have many ideas about who this person is, including:

* God,
* the Holy Spirit,
* the church,
* the good news about Jesus (the gospel),
* Paul,
* the emperor,
* the Roman Empire, or
* the government.

Alternatively, the term may refer to something or someone who comes before the man of lawlessness, an agent of lawlessness active at that time ([2:7–8](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:7-2Thess2:8)). 

In Greek literature, this expression could describe being possessed by a demon (evil angel). This figure would prepare the way for the man of lawlessness when his time comes ([2:3](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:3), [8](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:8)).

* **Associated Passages:** 2 Thessalonians 2:6

* *the one who now restrains*: This might be someone opposing the man of lawlessness, someone possessed by a demon (evil angel), or Satan, the one who possesses ([2 Thessalonians 2:9](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:9); see study note on 2:6).

* Paul warns about the destruction of the lawless one (see [2:3](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:3)). In ancient writings, an *epiphany* was when a god appeared or showed divine power, inspiring worship.

## 2 Thessalonians 3:7 (id: 15241)

In the ancient world, people valued teaching by example. Paul set a good example by working to earn his own food ([3:8](https://ref.ly/2Thess3:8); [1 Thessalonians 2:9](https://ref.ly/1Thess2:9)).

* **Associated Passages:** 2 Thessalonians 3:7

However, he did not use that privilege ([1 Thessalonians 2:9](https://ref.ly/1Thess2:9); [2 Thessalonians 3:6–10](https://ref.ly/2Thess3:6-2Thess3:10)).

## 2 Thessalonians 3:15 (id: 15231)

*do not regard him as an enemy*: In the ancient world, people broke social ties with enemies and took revenge against them. The apostle Paul warns the Thessalonians that discipline against Christians who disobey should not lead to this kind of attack ([3:14](https://ref.ly/2Thess3:14)). Instead, the church should treat them like family members, and act in hope that they will correct their behavior ([1 Thessalonians 5:12](https://ref.ly/1Thess5:12), [14](https://ref.ly/1Thess5:14)).

* **Associated Passages:** 2 Thessalonians 3:15

Paul was always aware of the presence of the Lord Jesus with his people ([Romans 15:33](https://ref.ly/Rom15:33); [Philippians 4:9](https://ref.ly/Phil4:9)).

## 2 Thessalonians 3:17 (id: 15233)

A secretary wrote this letter for Paul ([Romans 16:22](https://ref.ly/Rom16:22); [1 Peter 5:12](https://ref.ly/1Pet5:12)). Now Paul picks up the pen to add a final greeting written by his own hand (see [1 Corinthians 16:21](https://ref.ly/1Cor16:21); [Galatians 6:11](https://ref.ly/Gal6:11); [Colossians 4:18](https://ref.ly/Col4:18); [Philemon 1:19](https://ref.ly/Phlm1:19)). He does this because he suspects someone is using his name and circulating a letter he did not write ([2 Thessalonians 2:2](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:2)). He emphasizes this step to prove the letter is truly from him.

* **Associated Passages:** 2 Thessalonians 3:17
