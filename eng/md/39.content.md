## Malachi 1:1 (id: 21144)

*This is the burden of the word* (literally *An Oracle: The message*) *of the LORD to Israel*: This introduction shows that the book of Malachi is an important message from God. It identifies the author and audience but not the date or reason. The word *oracle* gives Malachi's message authority and urgency. His audience was expected to listen and respond.

* **Associated Passages:** Malachi 1:1

## Malachi 1:2 (id: 21146)

*loved*: The relationship between the Lord and Israel involves covenant implications. This love can mean God's choice or *election* of Israel as his people. Malachi's message shows that other aspects of God's unconditional covenant love for Israel, like his patient mercy (see [Malachi 3:6](https://ref.ly/Mal3:6), [17](https://ref.ly/Mal3:17)), are still active. See also [Romans 9:13](https://ref.ly/Rom9:13).

* **Associated Passages:** Malachi 1:2

## Malachi 1:2–5 (id: 21145)

Malachi’s sermons use a literary form called *disputation* (a structured argument). This form sounds like a courtroom scene and gives the message the tone of a trial. In the first disputation, the prophet states the central truth that God loves Israel ([Malachi 1:2](https://ref.ly/Mal1:2)). 

After this, the prophet challenges the people and responds to their objections. He develops this main claim through five additional messages that follow.

* **Associated Passages:** Malachi 1:2-5

## Malachi 1:3 (id: 21147)

* *hated* (the opposite of *loved* in [Malachi 1:2](https://ref.ly/Mal1:2)): Love and hate are opposite words in Old Testament legal and prophetic texts (see [Deuteronomy 7:9–10](https://ref.ly/Deut7:9-Deut7:10); [Amos 5:15](https://ref.ly/Amos5:15)). They often describe the separation caused by a broken covenant. Here, God says he rejected Esau and his descendants, the Edomites. Esau had despised and rejected a covenant relationship with the Lord (see [Genesis 25:34](https://ref.ly/Gen25:34); [26:34–35](https://ref.ly/Gen26:34-Gen26:35)).
* Esau was the ancestor of the Edomite nation. His inheritance, the territory of Edom, was on the southeastern edge of the Dead Sea. The mention of Jacob and Esau reminds us of the twin brothers’ rivalry ([Genesis 25:23–26](https://ref.ly/Gen25:23-Gen25:26)).

* **Associated Passages:** Malachi 1:3

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** To Cause to Possess (ID: `keyterm:Possess`); Edom (ID: `place:Edom`); Edomites (ID: `group:Edom`); Jackal (ID: `fauna:Jackal`)

## Malachi 1:4 (id: 21148)

* *the LORD of Hosts* (Hebrew *Yahweh tseba’oth*): This name for God is important in Old Testament prophetic writings. *Hosts* are the angelic forces under God's command. This phrase emphasizes God's power, which cannot be defeated.
* *the Land of Wickedness*: Esau was selfish and showed disrespect for the Lord’s covenant symbols (see [Genesis 25:34](https://ref.ly/Gen25:34)). The nation of Edom came to represent the pride of a self\-centered life (see [Jeremiah 49:16](https://ref.ly/Jer49:16)). The Edomites also allied with Babylon in destroying Jerusalem (see [Psalm 137:7–9](https://ref.ly/Ps137:7-Ps137:9); [Obadiah 1:10](https://ref.ly/Obad1:10), [12](https://ref.ly/Obad1:12)).

## Malachi 2:11 (id: 21155)

*Judah has broken faith; an abomination has been committed in Israel and in Jerusalem*: Men were divorcing their wives to marry non\-Israelite women for economic gain. These marriages allowed Jewish men to join existing merchant groups and trade networks when they returned from Babylonia. Malachi compares this unfaithfulness to idol worship. 

In Israelite history, marrying foreign women often led to worshiping foreign gods. Loyalty was essential in Israel's agreements, both with God and in marriage. Divorce disrespects the unity of the marriage agreement ([Malachi 2:15](https://ref.ly/Mal2:15); see [Genesis 2:24](https://ref.ly/Gen2:24)).

## Malachi 3:13–4.3 (id: 21167)

Malachi’s final message contains two distinct but related speeches. The first emphasizes serving the Lord ([Malachi 3:13–18](https://ref.ly/Mal3:13-Mal3:18)). The second compares the fate of the wicked and the righteous ([Malachi 4:1–3](https://ref.ly/Mal4:1-Mal4:3))

Each speech ends with the messenger formula (see study note on Malachi 1:8). The prophet revisits themes from the fourth message ([Malachi 3:1–5](https://ref.ly/Mal3:1-Mal3:5)), emphasizing God's desire for honesty and faithfulness in worship, especially with the coming judgment on the day of the Lord. 

If Malachi is like a courtroom scene, this last argument is the sentencing. Although wickedness seems to win over righteousness and God seems slow to judge sin in the community, the coming day of the Lord will prove God's justice. The wicked will be separated from the righteous by the fire of God's judgment.

## Malachi 4:4–6 (id: 21182)

The book ends with a closing section that points to Moses and Elijah. These two figures are models of faithfulness to the Lord. Moses represents the Law, and Elijah represents the Prophets (see [Matthew 17:1–4](https://ref.ly/Matt17:1-Matt17:4)). They are held up as examples for Malachi’s audience to follow.

In ancient Hebrew practice, these two appeals may have functioned as added closing messages for the scroll that contained the 12 Minor Prophets (the shorter prophetic books). If this is the case, the first closing message ([Malachi 4:4](https://ref.ly/Mal4:4)) links the scroll to the Law of Moses. The second closing message ([Malachi 4:5–6](https://ref.ly/Mal4:5-Mal4:6)) connects the Minor Prophets to the Major Prophets (the longer books of Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Ezekiel).

## Malachi 4:6 (id: 21185)

* *he will turn the hearts of the fathers to their children*: Two key themes in Malachi and the Old Testament prophets are turning hearts and reconciliation. In the Old Testament, *turn* means repentance and a complete change of loyalties. Turning toward God results in reconciliation between generations (see [2 Corinthians 5:18–20](https://ref.ly/2Cor5:18-2Cor5:20)).
* *strike the land with a curse*: The word *curse* means total destruction (see [Deuteronomy 7:26](https://ref.ly/Deut7:26); [1 Samuel 15:18](https://ref.ly/1Sam15:18); [Zechariah 14:11](https://ref.ly/Zech14:11)). The people of Israel who ignored God's prophet would face complete destruction, like the Canaanites who came before them (for example, [Joshua 6:17](https://ref.ly/Josh6:17)).
