## Habakkuk 1:1 (id: 13002)

The word message (or *oracle*) shows the book as God's revelation through his prophet (compare [Nahum 1:1](https://ref.ly/Nah1:1); [Malachi 1:1](https://ref.ly/Mal1:1)).

* **Associated Passages:** Habakkuk 1:1

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Utterance (ID: `keyterm:Utterance`); Habakkuk (ID: `person:Habakkuk`); Have a Vision (ID: `keyterm:HaveAVision`)

## Habakkuk 1:2 (id: 13004)

*I call for help but You do not hear*: The call and answer theme in Scripture often shows the speaker's trust in God as a refuge or guide (see [Psalm 102:1–2](https://ref.ly/Ps102:1-Ps102:2)). It also indicates a close relationship between the believer and God ([Psalm 145:18](https://ref.ly/Ps145:18); [Isaiah 65:24](https://ref.ly/Isa65:24)).

* **Associated Passages:** Habakkuk 1:2

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)

## Habakkuk 1:2–4 (id: 13003)

To Habakkuk, God seemed unconcerned about the evil spread throughout society in Judah ([Habakkuk 1:3–4](https://ref.ly/Hab1:3-Hab1:4)). God also seemed silent when Habakkuk complained about this evil ([1:2](https://ref.ly/Hab1:2)).

* **Associated Passages:** Habakkuk 1:2-4

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`); Instruction (ID: `keyterm:Instruction`); Mischief (ID: `keyterm:Mischief`)

## Habakkuk 1:3 (id: 13005)

In Habakkuk’s time, destruction and violence were widespread in Judean society (see Habakkuk Book Introduction, “Setting”).

* **Associated Passages:** Habakkuk 1:3

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Mischief (ID: `keyterm:Mischief`)

## Habakkuk 2:3 (id: 13020)

## Habakkuk 2:4 (id: 13021)

* God knows when proud people live only for themselves. Their dishonest lives show that they are not righteous. They refuse God’s instruction and choose what seems right to them ([Proverbs 12:15](https://ref.ly/Prov12:15); [21:8](https://ref.ly/Prov21:8), [29](https://ref.ly/Prov21:29); [29:27](https://ref.ly/Prov29:27)). In contrast, the righteous stand in the right relationship with God. They accept God’s righteous standards and live them out in their daily lives.
* *faith*: Hebrew *’emunah*. This word translates the Hebrew *’emunah* ("steadfastness, trust"). In Genesis, the same Hebrew root (*’mn*) describes Abram’s trust in God ([Genesis 15:6](https://ref.ly/Gen15:6)). God transforms how people think and choose when they trust him so they can follow God’s holy standards faithfully. See also [Romans 1:16–17](https://ref.ly/Rom1:16-Rom1:17); [Galatians 3:11](https://ref.ly/Gal3:11); [Hebrews 10:37–39](https://ref.ly/Heb10:37-Heb10:39).

* **Associated Passages:** Habakkuk 2:4

## Habakkuk 2:20 (id: 13018)

Although Habakkuk focuses on God's redemption of his people, his imagery sometimes includes ideas from ancient Near Eastern stories about the gods. For example:

* pestilence and plague ([Habakkuk 3:5](https://ref.ly/Hab3:5))
* river and sea ([Habakkuk 3:8](https://ref.ly/Hab3:8))
* sun and moon ([Habakkuk 3:11](https://ref.ly/Hab3:11))

This imagery reminds Habakkuk's original audience that the Lord alone is the one true God who rules over all these things.

## Habakkuk 3:8–15 (id: 13039)

The Lord acts as a divine warrior for his people, using his power to save them. This picture of God as a divine warrior appears in both the Old Testament and the New Testament. 

In the Old Testament, it is seen in Israel’s escape from Egypt and their journey to Mount Sinai ([Exodus 15:1–18](https://ref.ly/Exod15:1-Exod15:18)). It also appears as Israel approached the promised land from the south and during their early victories in the land (for example, [Judges 5:4–5](https://ref.ly/Judg5:4-Judg5:5); [Psalms 18:8–16](https://ref.ly/Ps18:8-Ps18:16); [77:16–20](https://ref.ly/Ps77:16-Ps77:20)). 

A key New Testament example of this theme appears in Revelation, where Christ comes as a warrior to defeat evil and bring final justice ([Revelation 19:11–21](https://ref.ly/Rev19:11-Rev19:21)).

## Habakkuk 3:17–19 (id: 13030)

* *Though … yet I will exult*: Even if God never gives material blessings to his people again, he is still worthy of all their trust and praise. No matter what happens, the prophet could rejoice, knowing that the Lord is not only Israel’s Redeemer but also the source of his own salvation.
