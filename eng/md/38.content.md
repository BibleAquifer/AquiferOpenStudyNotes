## Zechariah 1:1 (id: 25978)

* This introduction labels the book of Zechariah as an *oracle*, which means it is an authoritative message inspired by God. The prophet Zechariah served as God's messenger, chosen to speak with God's authority.
* King Darius ruled from 521 to 486 BC, during the early years of the Persian Empire. The Persian Empire ruled a large part of the ancient world from 539 to 331 BC.

* **Associated Passages:** Zechariah 1:1

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Iddo (ID: `person:Iddo.6`); Berechiah (ID: `person:Berechiah.6`); Zechariah (ID: `person:Zechariah.17`); Word of the Lord (ID: `keyterm:WordOfTheLord`); LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`); Darius (ID: `person:Darius.2`)

## Zechariah 1:3 (id: 25981)

* the LORD of Hosts: God has an infinite number of angelic troops ready to do his will (see [2 Kings 6:17](https://ref.ly/2Kgs6:17); [Matthew 26:53](https://ref.ly/Matt26:53)). The phrase was probably meant to be an encouragement to the Judeans.
* *Return to me*: Repentance means a complete change. It is a move away from sin and self towards loyalty to God and his covenant (see [Isaiah 44:22](https://ref.ly/Isa44:22); [Malachi 3:7](https://ref.ly/Mal3:7); see also [Psalms 80:3–14](https://ref.ly/Ps80:3-Ps80:14); [85:4–8](https://ref.ly/Ps85:4-Ps85:8)).

* **Associated Passages:** Zechariah 1:3

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)

## Zechariah 1:4 (id: 25982)

* Before the exile in Babylon, earlier prophets urged Judah and Israel to repent. Zechariah shows the influence of Jeremiah and Ezekiel.
* *your fathers … they did not listen*: The people of Israel ([2 Kings 17:13–14](https://ref.ly/2Kgs17:13-2Kgs17:14)) and Judah ([2 Chronicles 36:15–16](https://ref.ly/2Chr36:15-2Chr36:16)) went into exile because they stubbornly ignored the word of the Lord (compare[2 Chronicles 30:7](https://ref.ly/2Chr30:7)).

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)

## Zechariah 1:8 (id: 25986)

* The vision during the night follows a standard pattern:

    + an introductory statement,
        + a description of the vision,
        + the prophet’s request for an interpretation, and
        + an angelic explanation.
* The myrtle trees were likely in the wooded Kidron Valley outside Jerusalem. The Lord had symbolically returned to the edge of the city. He had not yet entered Jerusalem because the temple was still being rebuilt.
* The colors of the red, brown, and white horses are not important to this vision’s meaning. These angelic riders patrolled the earth ([Zechariah 1:10](https://ref.ly/Zech1:10)), showing God’s ongoing concern for Jerusalem.

* **Associated Passages:** Zechariah 1:8

## Zechariah 3:1 (id: 26065)

* Jeshua was the high priest and the religious leader among those who returned to Judah from exile in Babylon ([Ezra 2:2](https://ref.ly/Ezra2:2)). Jeshua worked with Zerubbabel, the governor of the restored community. They worked together to rebuild the temple in Jerusalem ([Ezra 5:2](https://ref.ly/Ezra5:2); [Haggai 2:1–5](https://ref.ly/Hag2:1-Hag2:5)).
* *Satan* (literally *The satan*) is a member of the heavenly court. His role is to bring charges against human beings for wrongdoing. He speaks against them before God (see [Job 1:6](https://ref.ly/Job1:6)).

    This adversarial role could be carried out by either human or divine beings (see [Numbers 22:22](https://ref.ly/Num22:22); [1 Samuel 29:4](https://ref.ly/1Sam29:4); [1 Chronicles 21:1](https://ref.ly/1Chr21:1)).

    By New Testament times, *Satan* had become a personal name. It referred to the ruler of demons and the chief opponent of God’s work ([Mark 3:22](https://ref.ly/Mark3:22); [2 Corinthians 4:4](https://ref.ly/2Cor4:4); [Ephesians 6:11](https://ref.ly/Eph6:11); [Revelation 20:2](https://ref.ly/Rev20:2)).

* Satan's authority in the heavenly court is shown by his position at the angel’s right hand.

* **Associated Passages:** Zechariah 3:1

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Satan (ID: `deity:Satan`); Joshua (ID: `person:Joshua.5`); An Angel (ID: `deity:Angel`); LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)

## Zechariah 3:2–3 (id: 26066)

* The Lord takes both roles in the vision. He speaks in defense of Jeshua and also gives the final decision. He rejects the accusations, ends the case, and declares Jeshua innocent because God has chosen and cleansed him.
* Like a burning stick snatched from the fire, God saved Jeshua and other Hebrew exiles from near destruction in Babylon.
* Jeshua’s filthy clothes symbolize the guilt and pollution of sin that led to the divine judgment of exile ([Zechariah 3:4](https://ref.ly/Zech3:4)). Satan's accusations likely suggested that, just as an unclean priest was unfit for temple service, the Hebrews were unfit to be God's chosen people.

* **Associated Passages:** Zechariah 3:2-3

## Zechariah 3:8 (id: 26070)

* The high priest Jeshua and the other priests symbolized greater truths.

## Zechariah 3:9 (id: 26071)

* The *stone* represents authority. It may refer to the last stone of the temple placed by Zerubbabel ([Zechariah 4:7](https://ref.ly/Zech4:7)), the *cornerstone* of the rebuilt Jerusalem ([Isaiah 28:16](https://ref.ly/Isa28:16)), or the precious stones on the high priests' ephod ([Exodus 25:7](https://ref.ly/Exod25:7); [35:9](https://ref.ly/Exod35:9)).
* The seven parts (*seven eyes*) of the stone suggest perfect and complete knowledge and wisdom. They might connect to the seven lamps of the golden lampstand ([Zechariah 4:2](https://ref.ly/Zech4:2)) or to God’s *seven spirits* ([Revelation 1:4](https://ref.ly/Rev1:4)).
* The inscription recalls the names of the tribes engraved on the high priest’s shoulder\-pieces ([Exodus 39:6](https://ref.ly/Exod39:6)).
* The removal of Israel’s sin in one day refers to the day of atonement ([Leviticus 16](https://ref.ly/Lev16:1-Lev16:34)). It points ahead to the death of Jesus on the cross, when the Lamb of God would take away the sin of the world ([John 1:29](https://ref.ly/John1:29)).
