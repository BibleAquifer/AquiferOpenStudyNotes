## Revelation 1:1 (id: 25181)

* The word *revelation* (Greek *apokalupsis*) introduces the book's visionary style as apocalyptic writing (see Revelation Book Introduction, “Apocalyptic Writing”). God shares his message through mysterious symbols, numbers, and word pictures.
* *of* *Jesus Christ*: He is both the source and the main subject of the book.
* *what must soon come to pass*: Compare [Revelation 3:11](https://ref.ly/Rev3:11); [22:6–7](https://ref.ly/Rev22:6-Rev22:7); [Luke 18:8](https://ref.ly/Luke18:8); [Romans 16:20](https://ref.ly/Rom16:20).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:1

## Revelation 1:2 (id: 25183)

*testifies* … *testimony*: The Greek words behind these terms are *martureō* (meaning “to bear witness”) and *marturia* (meaning “witness” or “testimony”). These related Greek words refer to a true and reliable witness. They highlight that the sacrificial witness of Jesus and the true testimony of John form the foundation for the revelation.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:2

## Revelation 1:3 (id: 25184)

* *blessed is the one*: Revelation includes seven promises of blessing ([Revelation 1:3](https://ref.ly/Rev1:3); [14:13](https://ref.ly/Rev14:13); [16:15](https://ref.ly/Rev16:15); [19:9](https://ref.ly/Rev19:9); [20:6](https://ref.ly/Rev20:6); [22:7](https://ref.ly/Rev22:7), [14](https://ref.ly/Rev22:14)).
* *hear and obey*: True listening means you follow what you hear.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:3

## Revelation 1:4 (id: 25186)

* *To the seven churches* do not mean there were only seven churches in the Roman province of Asia. Instead, these seven churches represent the entire group. "Grace and peace" is a typical Christian greeting (see study notes on 2 Corinthians 1:1–2; 2 Thessalonians 1:1–2). The order of these words is consistent in the New Testament, suggesting that peace follows from God's grace.
* *from Him who is and was and is to come*: God controlled the past, will control the future, and has complete authority and power over every present crisis (see also [Revelation 1:8](https://ref.ly/Rev1:8); [4:8](https://ref.ly/Rev4:8); [11:17](https://ref.ly/Rev11:17); [16:5](https://ref.ly/Rev16:5)).
* *the seven spirits*: Some argue that the "seven spirits" are seven angels, but the phrase fits between references to God the Father ([Revelation 1:4](https://ref.ly/Rev1:4)) and God the Son ([1:5](https://ref.ly/Rev1:5)), making this passage a description of the Trinity (see [Matthew 28:19](https://ref.ly/Matt28:19); [John 14:26](https://ref.ly/John14:26); [15:26](https://ref.ly/John15:26); [2 Corinthians 13:14](https://ref.ly/2Cor13:14); [1 Peter 1:2](https://ref.ly/1Pet1:2)). The number seven acknowledges the holy spirit’s perfection (compare [Zechariah 4:2](https://ref.ly/Zech4:2), [6](https://ref.ly/Zech4:6), [10](https://ref.ly/Zech4:10)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:4
* **Associated Articles:** 2 Corinthians 1:1-2 (ID: `14257@TyndaleStudyNotes`); 2 Thessalonians 1:1-2 (ID: `15199@TyndaleStudyNotes`)

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Asia (ID: `place:Asia`)

## Revelation 1:5 (id: 25188)

* Jesus serves as our example for sharing the good of salvation ([Revelation 1:2](https://ref.ly/Rev1:2)).
* The belief that Jesus was the first to rise from the dead is essential to Christian faith ([1 Corinthians 15:14](https://ref.ly/1Cor15:14), [17](https://ref.ly/1Cor15:17)).
* As the ruler of all the kings of the world (see also [Revelation 17:14](https://ref.ly/Rev17:14); [19:16](https://ref.ly/Rev19:16)), he is the ultimate Lord of everything. People in the Roman world were expected to declare Caesar as the absolute lord; many early Christians died for their belief that Jesus alone holds that position.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:5

## Revelation 1:5–6 (id: 25187)

* In this doxology, John explains why we should praise Jesus.
* A doxology is a short statement that gives praise to God or to Jesus. These statements often end with the word *Amen* (see [Romans 11:36](https://ref.ly/Rom11:36); [Philippians 4:20](https://ref.ly/Phil4:20)), which expresses agreement and affirmation.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:5-6

## Revelation 1:8 (id: 25189)

*I am* is the name of God in the Old Testament ([Exodus 3:13–14](https://ref.ly/Exod3:13-Exod3:14); [6:2](https://ref.ly/Exod6:2); [Deuteronomy 6:4](https://ref.ly/Deut6:4)). Jesus used this name for himself (compare [John 4:26](https://ref.ly/John4:26)). Alpha and Omega are the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet. They show that God's actions cover everything. God is completely in control.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:8

## Revelation 1:9 (id: 25191)

* *your brother and partner*: Although separated from other Christians due to imprisonment, John felt a sense of community with them in suffering. This connection was based on hope in God's kingdom and a willingness to endure.
* The Roman fortress on the island of Patmos held prisoners and exiles. Patmos was part of a group of islands that protected the busy seaport of Miletus.
* *because of the word of God and my testimony*: These two statements mean the same thing. This is a common pattern in Semitic writing, where the second line repeats or explains the first. By John's time, Jews were convincing Roman authorities that Christianity was different from Judaism and therefore not an authorized religion.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:9

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Patmos (ID: `place:Patmos`)

## Revelation 1:10 (id: 25170)

* *the Lord’s day*: In the Roman world, this term referred to celebrations for Caesar. Christians, however, used it for their weekly worship, celebrating the resurrection of Jesus. The earliest Christians worshiped in Jewish synagogues on the Sabbath, the last day of the week ([Acts 18:4](https://ref.ly/Acts18:4)). By the time of Revelation, they were no longer allowed in synagogues and gathered on the first day of the week, which marked the resurrection (see study note on Luke 24:1; see also [Acts 20:7](https://ref.ly/Acts20:7)).
* The loud voice like a trumpet blast announced the Lord’s coming.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:10
* **Associated Articles:** Luke 24:1 (ID: `20901@TyndaleStudyNotes`)

## Revelation 1:13 (id: 25174)

* *the Son of Man* (or *a son of man*): *Son of Man* is a title Jesus used for himself (see [Daniel 7:13](https://ref.ly/Dan7:13); [Matthew 8:20](https://ref.ly/Matt8:20); [9:6](https://ref.ly/Matt9:6); [16:13](https://ref.ly/Matt16:13); [Mark 2:28](https://ref.ly/Mark2:28); [9:9](https://ref.ly/Mark9:9); [14:41](https://ref.ly/Mark14:41); [John 1:51](https://ref.ly/John1:51); [3:13](https://ref.ly/John3:13); [5:27](https://ref.ly/John5:27)).
* The long robe and gold sash give Christ an authoritative look (see [Revelation 19:13–16](https://ref.ly/Rev19:13-Rev19:16); [Daniel 10:5](https://ref.ly/Dan10:5)). The robe was a priestly garment symbolizing purity and holiness.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:13

## Revelation 1:14 (id: 25175)

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:14

## Revelation 1:15 (id: 25176)

* The thundering voice shows God's power. When God speaks, people will listen (compare [Ezekiel 43:2](https://ref.ly/Ezek43:2)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:15

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Kiln (ID: `realia:Kiln`)
## Revelation 1:16 (id: 25177)

* The seven stars symbolize the complete church. The church is safe in the right hand of the Son of Man, which means it is under his care, acceptance, and blessing.
* The sharp sword coming from his mouth represents his powerful message and judgment (see [Revelation 19:15](https://ref.ly/Rev19:15); [Genesis 3:24](https://ref.ly/Gen3:24); [Ephesians 6:17](https://ref.ly/Eph6:17); [Hebrews 4:12](https://ref.ly/Heb4:12)). Jesus announces both grace and judgment, but here the focus is on his role in carrying out judgment (see [Revelation 2:12](https://ref.ly/Rev2:12), [16](https://ref.ly/Rev2:16); [19:15](https://ref.ly/Rev19:15), [21](https://ref.ly/Rev19:21); compare [Isaiah 11:4](https://ref.ly/Isa11:4)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:16

## Revelation 1:17 (id: 25178)

* *When I saw Him, I fell … dead*: Terror and fainting, if not death itself (see [Exodus 33:20](https://ref.ly/Exod33:20)), often occur when someone sees God (see [Isaiah 6:1–5](https://ref.ly/Isa6:1-Isa6:5); [Matthew 28:4](https://ref.ly/Matt28:4)). The Lord kindly responds by touching with his right hand (compare [Matthew 25:34](https://ref.ly/Matt25:34)) and saying, *Do not be afraid* (compare [Luke 1:13](https://ref.ly/Luke1:13)), showing both grace and acceptance.
* *I am*: This phrase identifies Jesus as God (see study note on Revelation 1:8).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:17
* **Associated Articles:** Revelation 1:8 (ID: `25189@TyndaleStudyNotes`)

## Revelation 1:18 (id: 25179)

Jesus has full authority to offer hope in a hostile world.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:18

## Revelation 1:19 (id: 25180)

*Write*: In the vision, God instructs John on what to do for him (see [Isaiah 6:9–10](https://ref.ly/Isa6:9-Isa6:10)). This vision aims to help the church understand the present and future from God's viewpoint ([Revelation 1:20](https://ref.ly/Rev1:20)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:19

## Revelation 1:20 (id: 25182)

* John must record what he sees because Revelation is an important message for the churches.
* The *angels* (or *messengers;* Greek *angelos*) of the seven churches could be:

    1. the guardian angels of these churches (compare [Matthew 18:10](https://ref.ly/Matt18:10); [Acts 12:15](https://ref.ly/Acts12:15));
        2. the leading officials of the churches (compare [Matthew 11:10](https://ref.ly/Matt11:10), where “messenger” is *angelos*); or
        3. a personification of the *ethos* (spirit or character) of the church.
        The best understanding is probably a combination of 1 and 3\.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 1:20

## Revelation 2:1 (id: 25363)

* *To the angel … write*: This repeated command introducing each of the seven letters shows the message's importance.
* Ephesus became the main city of the Roman province of Asia. It had the temple of Artemis (see [Acts 19:23–35](https://ref.ly/Acts19:23-Acts19:35)) and became the richest banking center in that region. The people of Ephesus were very independent. They refused help from Alexander to rebuild their temple after its destruction in the 300s BC. They argued that one god should not build a temple for another god (see Strabo, *Geography* 14\.1\.22\). The pride of the Ephesians also led to the uproar against Paul ([Acts 19:23–41](https://ref.ly/Acts19:23-Acts19:41)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:1

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Ephesus (ID: `place:Ephesus`)

## Revelation 2:2–3 (id: 25364)

* *I know*: This repeated refrain ([Revelation 2:9](https://ref.ly/Rev2:9), [13](https://ref.ly/Rev2:13), [19](https://ref.ly/Rev2:19); [3:1](https://ref.ly/Rev3:1), [8](https://ref.ly/Rev3:8), [15](https://ref.ly/Rev3:15)) shows Christ knows everything about his people, their actions, and their situations.
* The Ephesian Christians had a correct understanding of their beliefs, marked by perseverance and faithfulness. They examined various claims, disciplined evil people, could tell what is true and false, and patiently suffered for their faith in Christ.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:2-3

## Revelation 2:4 (id: 25370)

*You have abandoned your first love*: When the church began, their love for Christ and each other was strong. Struggles with false teachers and persecution caused that original love to grow cold. Correct beliefs, action, and even suffering ([Revelation 2:2–3](https://ref.ly/Rev2:2-Rev2:3)) are only an empty form of Christian life if living and active love is missing ([1 Corinthians 13](https://ref.ly/1Cor13:1-1Cor13:13)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:4

## Revelation 2:5 (id: 25371)

Christ calls those who keep the faith to return to him and repent of being cold\-hearted. The warning, I will come and remove your lampstand, means they would lose their status as a church. God would treat them like he did the people who abandoned their faith within Israel (see [Romans 11](https://ref.ly/Rom11:1-Rom11:36)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:5

## Revelation 2:6 (id: 25372)

Little is known about the Nicolaitans. Their teachings ([Revelation 2:15](https://ref.ly/Rev2:15)) suggest they ate food offered to idols and engaged in sexual immorality. The council in Jerusalem prohibited this behavior (see [Acts 15:20](https://ref.ly/Acts15:20), [29](https://ref.ly/Acts15:29)). The early church father Irenaeus, writing around AD 180, said that the Nicolaitans followed the teaching of Nicolas ([Acts 6:3–5](https://ref.ly/Acts6:3-Acts6:5)). He believed John's writings opposed the Nicolaitan false teachings linked to Cerinthus (see 1 John Book Introduction, “Setting”).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:6

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Nicolaitans (ID: `group:Nicolaitans`)
## Revelation 2:7 (id: 25373)

Obeying leads to the reward of eternal life, symbolized by fruit from the tree of life ([Revelation 22:2](https://ref.ly/Rev22:2); [Genesis 3:22](https://ref.ly/Gen3:22)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:7

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:8

## Revelation 2:8–11 (id: 25374)

## Revelation 2:9 (id: 25376)

* Jesus linked material poverty with the blessing of being rich in God's Kingdom ([Matthew 5:3](https://ref.ly/Matt5:3), [10–12](https://ref.ly/Matt5:10-Matt5:12); [Luke 6:20](https://ref.ly/Luke6:20)).
* Jews without faith are criticized for siding with Satan against Christianity ([Revelation 3:9](https://ref.ly/Rev3:9); see [John 8:44](https://ref.ly/John8:44); [Acts 14:2–5](https://ref.ly/Acts14:2-Acts14:5); [17:13](https://ref.ly/Acts17:13); [18:6](https://ref.ly/Acts18:6); [20:3](https://ref.ly/Acts20:3); [Galatians 5:11](https://ref.ly/Gal5:11); [1 Thessalonians 2:14–16](https://ref.ly/1Thess2:14-1Thess2:16)). At the Jewish council of Jamnia, Jewish leaders decided that Christians were unholy and should be excluded. John was not speaking against Jewish people as a whole. He was a Jew describing the actions of some of his fellow Jews toward both Jewish and non\-Jewish Christians.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:9

## Revelation 2:10 (id: 25353)

* John saw the devil as the source of human hostility against Christians.
* The period of their suffering would last ten days, symbolizing a limited time of persecution (see [1 Peter 1:6](https://ref.ly/1Pet1:6)). If they remained faithful, their reward would be the crown of eternal life.
* even unto death: John’s student Polycarp became a martyr in Smyrna in the mid\-100s AD.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:10

## Revelation 2:11 (id: 25354)

## Revelation 2:12–17 (id: 25355)

The letter to the church in Pergamum describes Christians facing temptations to compromise their morals and loyalty to God. Pergamum was the first capital of the Roman province of Asia. The city had a famous library, and its people developed the use of animal skins for writing.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:12-17

## Revelation 2:13 (id: 25357)

* The throne of Satan might refer to the altar of Zeus on the mountain above the city or to emperor worship at the temple of Augustus. For many years, the Roman governor had his throne there, and the great temple of Athena and other shrines were also in Pergamum. Calling it Satan’s city might also refer to the temple of Asclepius, whose symbol was coiled snakes. The city was dedicated to the Roman gods and emperor worship.
* Antipas is otherwise unknown.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 2:13

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Antipas (ID: `person:Antipas`)

## Revelation 2:17 (id: 25359)

* Those who stay faithful to Christ will receive manna, which is nourishment from heaven (see [Exodus 16:11–36](https://ref.ly/Exod16:11-Exod16:36)).

The city was on the Via Maris, the main highway between Egypt and Mesopotamia. Many armies used this route, making the site known for bloody battles. For example, Pharaoh Neco killed Josiah here on his way to fight the Assyrians ([2 Kings 23:29](https://ref.ly/2Kgs23:29)). The twelve precious stones on the city's wall foundation remind us of the twelve stones on the high priest's breastplate. These stones represented God's people (see [Exodus 28:17–20](https://ref.ly/Exod28:17-Exod28:20)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 21:19

## Revelation 21:21 (id: 25402)

In heaven, common building materials are the things we consider precious and luxurious on earth.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 21:21

## Revelation 21:22 (id: 25403)

*I saw no temple*: This seems to conflict with earlier visions ([Revelation 11:19](https://ref.ly/Rev11:19); [14:15–17](https://ref.ly/Rev14:15-Rev14:17)). However, each vision in Revelation symbolically represents God's presence. There is no need for a temple in heaven because God is with his people.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 21:22

## Revelation 21:23 (id: 25404)

* The city's vitality, energy, and life come from the glory of God and the Lamb, not from institutions or physical sources of power and light. The sun and moon were part of the first creation ([Genesis 1:14–19](https://ref.ly/Gen1:14-Gen1:19); see [Isaiah 60:19–20](https://ref.ly/Isa60:19-Isa60:20)).
* *The Lamb is its lamp*: Jesus is the light of the world (see [John 8:12](https://ref.ly/John8:12); [9:5](https://ref.ly/John9:5); [12:35–36](https://ref.ly/John12:35-John12:36)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 21:23

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Glory (ID: `keyterm:Glory`)
## Revelation 21:24–25 (id: 25405)

* *the nations*: God’s promise to Abraham comes true ([Genesis 12:3](https://ref.ly/Gen12:3); see [Isaiah 2:3](https://ref.ly/Isa2:3)).
* In Revelation, the kings of the world often represent God’s enemies ([Revelation 6:15](https://ref.ly/Rev6:15); [17:18](https://ref.ly/Rev17:18); [18:3](https://ref.ly/Rev18:3), [9](https://ref.ly/Rev18:9)). Now that these enemies are destroyed ([Revelation 16:9](https://ref.ly/Rev16:9), [11](https://ref.ly/Rev16:11); [19:1–2](https://ref.ly/Rev19:1-Rev19:2), [19–21](https://ref.ly/Rev19:19-Rev19:21)), the image is of God’s glory shown in those who have repented ([Revelation 14:6–7](https://ref.ly/Rev14:6-Rev14:7); compare [11:13](https://ref.ly/Rev11:13)) and walk with God ([5:9](https://ref.ly/Rev5:9); [15:4](https://ref.ly/Rev15:4)).
* The gates that are never closed contrast with ancient times when closed gates protected cities from enemies, especially at night. The people experience peace and security under God’s rule (see [Isaiah 60:11](https://ref.ly/Isa60:11)). God’s enemies are gone, and fear and failure have ended (see [Zechariah 14:7](https://ref.ly/Zech14:7); [John 11:8–10](https://ref.ly/John11:8-John11:10); [12:35–36](https://ref.ly/John12:35-John12:36)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 21:24-25

## Revelation 21:27 (id: 25406)

*unclean*: Spiritual impurity is a key concern in Revelation (see [Revelation 21:8](https://ref.ly/Rev21:8)). Here, evil includes idolatry and dishonesty (see [Revelation 14:5](https://ref.ly/Rev14:5); [21:8](https://ref.ly/Rev21:8); [22:15](https://ref.ly/Rev22:15)). Those not listed in the *Book of Life* cannot enter the city of God. Only those with names in the *Book of Life*, the pure of heart ([Matthew 5:8](https://ref.ly/Matt5:8)), will see God.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 21:27

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:1

## Revelation 22:1–2 (id: 25414)

This part of John's vision of the city shows how God provides water and food for his people (compare [Exodus 16:4](https://ref.ly/Exod16:4), [22–24](https://ref.ly/Exod16:22-Exod16:24); [Ezekiel 47:1–12](https://ref.ly/Ezek47:1-Ezek47:12); [John 4:10](https://ref.ly/John4:10); [6:32–35](https://ref.ly/John6:32-John6:35)). God first created a "garden of delight" (Eden) for Adam and Eve, but they disobeyed him and lost it ([Genesis 2–3](https://ref.ly/Gen2:1-Gen3:24)). Now, Eden is remade and joined with the heavenly city as God's wonderful gift for his faithful people.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:1-2

## Revelation 22:2 (id: 25426)

After humans sinned, they lost access to the tree of life (see [Genesis 3:22–24](https://ref.ly/Gen3:22-Gen3:24)), but now it is freely available. The tree stands on each side of the river (compare [Ezekiel 47:12](https://ref.ly/Ezek47:12)), showing there is no wrong side of the river in heaven. The tree produces fresh fruit every month, demonstrating God's constant provision.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:2

## Revelation 22:3 (id: 25427)

* *No longer … any curse*: Since the first sin ([Genesis 3:14–19](https://ref.ly/Gen3:14-Gen3:19)), humanity has faced a curse due to their rebellion against God (see [1 Corinthians 16:22](https://ref.ly/1Cor16:22)). Now, rebellion, sin, and the curse are gone forever.
* *His servants will worship him*: Revelation shows examples of true worship (see [Revelation 4–5](https://ref.ly/Rev4:1-Rev5:14); [7:9–17](https://ref.ly/Rev7:9-Rev7:17)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:3

## Revelation 22:4 (id: 25428)

The fear of death and seeing God will disappear (see [Genesis 16:13](https://ref.ly/Gen16:13); [32:30](https://ref.ly/Gen32:30); [Judges 6:22](https://ref.ly/Judg6:22); [Isaiah 6:5](https://ref.ly/Isa6:5)). God's people will carry his name and joyfully see his face (see [Matthew 5:8](https://ref.ly/Matt5:8); [2 Corinthians 3:18](https://ref.ly/2Cor3:18); [1 John 3:2–3](https://ref.ly/1John3:2-1John3:3)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:4

## Revelation 22:5 (id: 25429)

They will rule with God forever (see [Revelation 20:4](https://ref.ly/Rev20:4); see also [5:10](https://ref.ly/Rev5:10)) in his bright light that removes night and the need for lamps (see [Isaiah 60:19–20](https://ref.ly/Isa60:19-Isa60:20)).

## Revelation 22:6 (id: 25431)

Everything John records in Revelation is trustworthy and true because God, who has all authority (compare [Matthew 28:18](https://ref.ly/Matt28:18)), sent the messenger. However, these visions are not easy to understand.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:6

## Revelation 22:6–21 (id: 25430)

This epilogue to Revelation includes words from an angel ([Revelation 22:6](https://ref.ly/Rev22:6), [8–11](https://ref.ly/Rev22:8-Rev22:11)) and Christ ([22:7](https://ref.ly/Rev22:7), [12–19](https://ref.ly/Rev22:12-Rev22:19)). It ends with a plea for Christ’s return ([Revelation 22:20](https://ref.ly/Rev22:20)) and a final blessing ([22:21](https://ref.ly/Rev22:21)). The epilogue connects directly with the book's introduction ([Revelation 1:1–11](https://ref.ly/Rev1:1-Rev1:11)). It highlights key themes such as: 

* encouraging faithful perseverance,
* warning those who do evil things,
* affirming the truth of the prophetic message, and
* restating the nearness of Christ’s return.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:6-21

The warning against false worship (see [Revelation 19:10](https://ref.ly/Rev19:10)) is repeated when John falls at the angel's feet again. Only God deserves our worship (compare John the Baptist's relationship to Jesus; [John 1:6–8](https://ref.ly/John1:6-John1:8), [26–27](https://ref.ly/John1:26-John1:27); [3:27–35](https://ref.ly/John3:27-John3:35)).

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:8-9

## Revelation 22:10 (id: 25415)

*Do not seal up*: The angel expands John's initial instruction to *Write on a scroll* ([Revelation 1:11](https://ref.ly/Rev1:11)) and warns against limiting its communication. Although some things are sealed, God does not reveal everything (compare [Revelation 10:4](https://ref.ly/Rev10:4)). What has been written must be shared so people understand the message's seriousness and that the time is near (see [Revelation 1:1](https://ref.ly/Rev1:1), [3](https://ref.ly/Rev1:3); [22:6–7](https://ref.ly/Rev22:6-Rev22:7); compare [Daniel 12:4](https://ref.ly/Dan12:4)). 

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:10

## Revelation 22:11 (id: 25416)

People who refuse to accept God's forgiveness will remain wicked (see [Revelation 16:9](https://ref.ly/Rev16:9)). John reminds good people to keep their integrity. This verse warns wrongdoers and urges the righteous to understand the importance of Christ's return for their commitments and actions.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:11

## Revelation 22:12 (id: 25417)

Christ is coming soon to repay everyone, not just based on their faith, but according to their actions (see [Revelation 2:23](https://ref.ly/Rev2:23); [11:18](https://ref.ly/Rev11:18); [14:13](https://ref.ly/Rev14:13); [20:12–13](https://ref.ly/Rev20:12-Rev20:13); see also [Proverbs 24:12](https://ref.ly/Prov24:12); [Matthew 16:27](https://ref.ly/Matt16:27); [2 Corinthians 5:10](https://ref.ly/2Cor5:10)). Not every statement of belief is sincere ([John 2:23–25](https://ref.ly/John2:23-John2:25)); true faith shows through actions ([James 2:14–26](https://ref.ly/Jas2:14-Jas2:26)). We are saved by God's grace and power in Christ. Our actions do not save us, but they show the seriousness of our confession and provide a fair basis for our final reward or punishment.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:12

## Revelation 22:15 (id: 25419)

## Revelation 22:18–19 (id: 25422)

* *I testify*: John swears to protect the integrity of the Book of Revelation. He curses anyone who changes the book's content or message (compare [Deuteronomy 4:2](https://ref.ly/Deut4:2); [12:32](https://ref.ly/Deut12:32)). This curse contrasts with the blessing for those who read aloud, listen to, and follow the prophecy ([Revelation 1:3](https://ref.ly/Rev1:3)). When Revelation was written, scribes sometimes changed books to fit their views. Early Christians quickly developed ways to verify messages and messengers (see [John 21:24](https://ref.ly/John21:24); [1 Corinthians 16:21](https://ref.ly/1Cor16:21); [Galatians 6:11](https://ref.ly/Gal6:11); [Colossians 4:18](https://ref.ly/Col4:18); [2 Thessalonians 3:17](https://ref.ly/2Thess3:17); [1 John 4:1–3](https://ref.ly/1John4:1-1John4:3)).
* Since the early church, the New Testament's scope and content have defined Christian teachings. However, some still try to change Scripture by adding works they consider equal to the Bible or by claiming parts of the Bible are unreliable. The true church has rejected and will continue to reject these attempts to change the canon, viewing them as human or even demonic efforts to alter the foundation of the Christian faith.

* **Associated Passages:** Revelation 22:18-19
