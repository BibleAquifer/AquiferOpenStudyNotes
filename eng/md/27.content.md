## Daniel 1:1 (id: 9883)

Jehoiakim was king of Judah from 609 to 598 BC. Before this siege, he served Egypt as a vassal (a ruler who had to obey a stronger nation). His rule in Judah was very wicked ([2 Kings 23:36–24:6](https://ref.ly/2Kgs23:36-2Kgs24:6); [2 Chronicles 36:5–8](https://ref.ly/2Chr36:5-2Chr36:8); [Jeremiah 25](https://ref.ly/Jer25:1-Jer25:38)). The exile and captivity of Daniel and his friends in 605 BC foreshadowed the exile of the rest of the nation in 597 and 586 BC (see [2 Kings 24:1–25:21](https://ref.ly/2Kgs24:1-2Kgs25:21)). The Lord used King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon as an unknowing servant to achieve his purposes (compare [Jeremiah 25:9](https://ref.ly/Jer25:9); [27:6](https://ref.ly/Jer27:6)).

## Daniel 1:2 (id: 9886)

* The Lord, who has complete power and authority over history, gave the victory to Nebuchadnezzar. It was not accomplished by the king’s might or the power of his god Marduk, the main god of the Babylonians. The Lord used the exile in Babylon to judge and discipline his people, as he had warned in the Law (for example, [Deuteronomy 28:64](https://ref.ly/Deut28:64)) and by the prophets (for example, [Jeremiah 25:8–14](https://ref.ly/Jer25:8-Jer25:14)).
* The sacred objects were dedicated for use in the temple of God built by Solomon. After nearly seventy years in Marduk's treasure\-house, Belshazzar further defiled these vessels in 539 BC ([Daniel 5:1–4](https://ref.ly/Dan5:1-Dan5:4)). They were later returned to the rebuilt temple during Ezra's time ([Ezra 1:7–11](https://ref.ly/Ezra1:7-Ezra1:11); [5:13–16](https://ref.ly/Ezra5:13-Ezra5:16)).
* *the land of Shinar*: Israel's rebellion brought them back to where rebellious humanity had built the Tower of Babel ([Genesis 11:2](https://ref.ly/Gen11:2)).

## Daniel 1:4 (id: 9889)

* *without blemish*: They had no defects or blemishes that would disqualify them. The Lord required the same for Israel’s priests ([Leviticus 21:16–24](https://ref.ly/Lev21:16-Lev21:24); [22:17–25](https://ref.ly/Lev22:17-Lev22:25)). These youths had the wisdom, knowledge, understanding, and character needed to work in high government positions.
* Babylon had a wide range of literature that covered many kinds of knowledge. It included:

    + wisdom and secret writings,
        + creation stories,
        + magic spells,
        + theogonies (origins and family trees of the gods),
        + legal texts,
        + ancient histories,
        + letters,
        + dream journals,
        + guides for interpreting visions, and
        + mathematical and astrological materials.
        The education of these young men, all in a foreign language, was as advanced as that of Moses in Egypt.

## Daniel 1:6–7 (id: 9891)

Their new Babylonian names were meant to help bring the young Hebrews into Babylonian culture. Daniel, whose name means "God is my Judge" or "God has judged," became Belteshazzar, which means "Bel \[a Babylonian god] protects his life." Hananiah, whose name means "the Lord is grace," became Shadrach, which may mean "command of Aku," another Babylonian god. Mishael, whose name means "who is God?" became Meshach, which means "who is Aku?" Azariah, whose name means "the Lord helps," became Abednego, which means "servant of Nego/Nebo," another Babylonian god.

## Daniel 1:8 (id: 9893)

*Daniel made up his mind that he would not defile himself*: The meat and wine from Nebuchadnezzar’s table were likely not allowed by Jewish standards. The Babylonians used meats like pork, which were unclean (see [Leviticus 11:1–23](https://ref.ly/Lev11:1-Lev11:23); [Deuteronomy 14:1–21](https://ref.ly/Deut14:1-Deut14:21)), and their meat and wine were probably offered to Babylonian gods. The other young Hebrews agreed with Daniel ([Daniel 1:12–16](https://ref.ly/Dan1:12-Dan1:16)). They trusted God and followed his commands, and God blessed them (compare [Deuteronomy 28:1–14](https://ref.ly/Deut28:1-Deut28:14)).

## Daniel 9:27 (id: 10125)

* *he*: Most interpreters think this pronoun refers to the ruler in [Daniel 9:26](https://ref.ly/Dan9:26). Some believe it refers to the anointed one in [Daniel 9:25–26](https://ref.ly/Dan9:25-Dan9:26), suggesting a prophecy about Christ. They say Christ made a covenant and ended the need for sacrifices through his death.

    However, the term for making a treaty implies forcing a covenant, likely through intimidation. Antiochus IV forced a covenant with Judea's people when he began ruling in 175 BC (compare [Daniel 11:23](https://ref.ly/Dan11:23); see *1 Maccabees* 1:10–15\). Other interpreters think this prophecy was fulfilled in AD 66–70\. Some believe it refers to a future ruler.

* The phrase *put an end* means a destructive action, not an act of salvation like the Messiah's death. In 167 BC, Antiochus IV ordered Judea's people to stop Jewish worship in the temple and practice pagan worship under threat of death (see *1 Maccabees* 1:41–63\). Similarly, Roman general Titus ended sacrifices in the temple by destroying it in AD 70\.
* *the abomination that causes desolation* (literally *an abomination of desolation*): Compare [Daniel 8:13](https://ref.ly/Dan8:13); [11:31](https://ref.ly/Dan11:31); [12:11](https://ref.ly/Dan12:11). This term (Hebrew *shiqqutsim meshomem*) might mock the Semitic name for Zeus (*Ba‘al Shamayim*, “Lord of the Heavens”). Idol\-gods are often called *shiqquts*, “abomination,” “desolation,” in the Bible (see [Deuteronomy 29:17](https://ref.ly/Deut29:17); [2 Chronicles 15:8](https://ref.ly/2Chr15:8); [Isaiah 66:3](https://ref.ly/Isa66:3); [Jeremiah 32:34](https://ref.ly/Jer32:34); [Ezekiel 20:7](https://ref.ly/Ezek20:7)), and the sound similarity between *shomem*, “devastation, destruction, desolation,” and *shamayim* is clear.

    In December 167 BC, Antiochus IV placed a Zeus statue on the temple altar and sacrificed unclean animals there. The same phrase (*abomination of desolation*) describes that altar and its sacrifices in *1 Maccabees* 1:54\. Jesus and several New Testament authors predicted a future desolating sacrilege ([Matthew 24:15](https://ref.ly/Matt24:15); [Mark 13:14](https://ref.ly/Mark13:14); [Luke 21:20](https://ref.ly/Luke21:20); [2 Thessalonians 2:3–4](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:3-2Thess2:4)).

    After Titus destroyed the Jerusalem temple in AD 70, the Romans placed their idols in the temple area and made sacrifices to their gods (see Josephus, *War* 6\.6\.1\). The apostle John noted that many antichrists had appeared ([1 John 2:18–23](https://ref.ly/1John2:18-1John2:23)) and that the antichrist's spirit was active in the world ([1 John 4:3](https://ref.ly/1John4:3)).

* The time of destruction is under God's control, and the fate of this defiler is certain (compare [Daniel 7:11](https://ref.ly/Dan7:11); [8:25](https://ref.ly/Dan8:25); [11:45](https://ref.ly/Dan11:45)). This is true for every ruler who opposes God and tries to destroy God's people.

* *what has been decreed must be accomplished*: Antiochus suddenly developed a painful disease and died in 163 BC in the mountains of Persia (see [Daniel 8:25](https://ref.ly/Dan8:25); *1 Maccabees* 4:52–53; 6:16; *2 Maccabees* 9:4, 28; 10:5\).

## Daniel 12:11–12 (id: 9943)

* *the abomination of desolation*: See [Daniel 8:13–14](https://ref.ly/Dan8:13-Dan8:14), [23–26](https://ref.ly/Dan8:23-Dan8:26); [11:29–35](https://ref.ly/Dan11:29-Dan11:35). See also study note on Daniel 9:27.
* God's people must wait and stay faithful when the end times come.
* *1,290 days … 1,335 days*: Some interpreters think these numbers relate to events during the reign of Antiochus IV Epiphanes. Others believe they are symbolic.

    The number 1,290 equals 43 lunar months of 30 days each, which is about three and a half years in the solar calendar. This might represent the final half of the last “set of seven” ([Daniel 9:27](https://ref.ly/Dan9:27)).

    The number 1,335 might mean an extra 45 days of waiting until the end. Those who endure the entire time, and then some, will see God act for his people.
