## Hosea 1:1 (id: 13362)

* *This is the word of the LORD*: The book of Hosea starts by stating that these are not just human words but a message from the one true God (compare [Joel 1:1](https://ref.ly/Joel1:1); [Micah 1:1](https://ref.ly/Mic1:1); [Zephaniah 1:1](https://ref.ly/Zeph1:1)).
* *Hosea son of Beeri*: Several other important people in the Old Testament are also named Hosea (or Hoshea; both names are spelled the same in Hebrew; [Numbers 13:8](https://ref.ly/Num13:8); [2 Kings 17:1](https://ref.ly/2Kgs17:1)). Hosea means “the Lord saves,” which is a fitting name for this prophet.
* *Jeroboam … king of Israel*: Hosea gave prophecies to the northern kingdom of Israel during the reigns of its last seven kings. However, only one of them, Jeroboam II, is mentioned here (see Hosea Book Introduction, “Setting”).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 1:1

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Beeri (ID: `person:Beeri.2`); Hosea (ID: `person:Hosea`)
## Hosea 1:2–3 (id: 13364)

*a prostitute*: Some find it hard to believe that God would tell a prophet to marry a prostitute. Because of this, many interpret this story symbolically. Early church members often thought the marriage did not actually happen. They saw it as an allegory, with Hosea representing the Lord and Gomer representing Israel. 

Today, most believe the marriage was real, but they have different views on Gomer’s job before marriage. Some think Gomer was a regular prostitute, while others believe she was a sacred prostitute in Canaanite fertility worship. Others think Gomer was a young woman who had not yet engaged in illicit sexual activity, but God knew she was likely to commit adultery and prostitution.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 1:2-3

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Gomer (ID: `person:Gomer.2`); Diblaim (ID: `person:Diblaim`); Hosea (ID: `person:Hosea`); Play the Prostitute (ID: `keyterm:PlayTheProstitute`)

## Hosea 1:7 (id: 13368)

For many years, the kingdom of Israel relied on its armies, horses, and charioteers for security. However, this confidence was misplaced. True safety and security come from God's power as the Lord.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 1:7

## Hosea 1:9 (id: 13369)

* The name Lo\-ammi delivers the harshest judgment because it suggests the end of Israel's covenant with the Lord. The title "my people," given to Israel when they obeyed the Lord their God ([Leviticus 26:12](https://ref.ly/Lev26:12); see also [Exodus 6:7](https://ref.ly/Exod6:7)), was now taken away due to their clear unfaithfulness.
* *I am not your God*: The Hebrew phrase can be translated as *I am not ‘*I Am*’ for you* (compare [Exodus 3:14](https://ref.ly/Exod3:14)). The third child's name prophetically states that the Israelites are outside the covenant and can no longer call upon the I Am.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 1:9

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Lo-ammi (ID: `person:Lo-ammi`)
## Hosea 1:10 (id: 13360)

* *the sand of the sea*: This promise of future population growth for God's people repeats the promise given to the patriarchs Abraham and Jacob ([Genesis 22:17](https://ref.ly/Gen22:17); [32:12](https://ref.ly/Gen32:12)).
* *they will be called ‘sons of the living God.’*: This phrase is unique in the Old Testament, but Paul quoted it ([Romans 9:26](https://ref.ly/Rom9:26)) to show that God had also called the gentiles (non\-Jewsish people) to salvation.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 1:10

## Hosea 1:10–11 (id: 13359)

These verses show a sudden change in Hosea’s prophecy from judgment to hope. In the next three verses, Hosea changes the names of Gomer’s three children from symbols of divine punishment on Israel to symbols of blessing and hope. This clearly shows God’s power to reverse situations from darkness to light, judgment to hope, and even death to life.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 1:10-11

## Hosea 1:11 (id: 13361)

* *Judah and of Israel will be gathered together*: For nearly 200 years, Israel and Judah were separate kingdoms due to political rivalry. When God restores his people, he will reunite these kingdoms under one leader, like in David's time. He will also end the curse of exile (see also [Ezekiel 37:15–28](https://ref.ly/Ezek37:15-Ezek37:28)).
* *the day of Jezreel*: Hosea said that in the future, God would restore Jezreel's original meaning. It will represent blessing and growth instead of judgment.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 1:11

## Hosea 2:1 (id: 13425)

* *My people*: God will restore the third child's name to its original meaning.
* *My loved one*: Israel will again receive God's love and compassion.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:1

## Hosea 2:2–23 (id: 13426)

*Rebuke your mother, rebuke her*: At first glance, the Lord, like an upset husband, seems to be giving a divorce to his unfaithful wife, Israel (see [Deuteronomy 24:1](https://ref.ly/Deut24:1)). However, as the passage continues, it becomes clear that God’s goal in this lawsuit is not divorce, but reconciliation ([Hosea 2:14–23](https://ref.ly/Hos2:14-Hos2:23)). 

God’s case against Israel aims to make Israel aware of her sin and offer her a chance to return to her true husband. The Lord’s desire for reconciliation with Israel is surprising because the law required the death penalty for an unfaithful spouse ([Deuteronomy 22:22](https://ref.ly/Deut22:22); see also [Genesis 38:24](https://ref.ly/Gen38:24); [Leviticus 21:9](https://ref.ly/Lev21:9)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:2-23

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Flax (ID: `flora:Flax`); Valley of Achor (ID: `place:AchorValley`); Vine (ID: `flora:Vine`); Love (ID: `keyterm:Love`); LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)
## Hosea 2:3 (id: 13429)

The Lord warns unfaithful Israel that unless she repents and returns to her covenant partner, he will expose her nakedness (see also [Hosea 2:10](https://ref.ly/Hos2:10)). Ezekiel also uses this image of judgment and shame ([Ezekiel 16:36–37](https://ref.ly/Ezek16:36-Ezek16:37)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:3

## Hosea 2:5 (id: 13430)

The only evidence needed to prove Israel’s unfaithfulness comes from her own words: *I will go after my lovers*. Israel’s lovers were the Canaanite fertility deities, especially the *baals* (local representations of the god Baal). The Israelites worshiped them because they believed these gods controlled nature's reproductive forces. The Israelites attributed to them the basic necessities of life: food, water, clothing of wool and linen, and oil used for both sacred and everyday purposes.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:5

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Flax (ID: `flora:Flax`)
## Hosea 2:6–7 (id: 13431)

*Therefore*: The initial Hebrew word of [Hosea 2:6](https://ref.ly/Hos2:6) (also of [2:9](https://ref.ly/Hos2:9) and [2:14](https://ref.ly/Hos2:14)) introduces God’s judgment on Israel. Because of her idolatry, the Lord would limit Israel’s access to the Canaanite fertility gods. Like an aggressive prostitute, she will chase her lovers, but she will not find them. In the end, Israel will realize it is better to return to her rightful husband.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:6-7

## Hosea 2:8 (id: 13432)

*she does not acknowledge* (literally *she does not know*): Israel needs to know God personally and through experience. If the Israelites had known the Lord this way, they would have understood that every good thing in their lives came from him, not from the *baals* (false gods).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:8

## Hosea 2:9–13 (id: 13433)

God's first judgment ([Hosea 2:6–7](https://ref.ly/Hos2:6-Hos2:7)) was to limit the Israelites. This second judgment would take away and destroy what he had given them.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:9-13

## Hosea 2:11 (id: 13418)

From the context ([Hosea 2:13](https://ref.ly/Hos2:13), [16–17](https://ref.ly/Hos2:16-Hos2:17)), and similar passages in [Isaiah 1:12–17](https://ref.ly/Isa1:12-Isa1:17) and [Amos 5:21–24](https://ref.ly/Amos5:21-Amos5:24), it is clear that the Israelites had corrupted their festivals, new moon celebrations, and Sabbath days. They mixed worship of the Lord with worship of the Canaanite fertility god, Baal. God rejects this *syncretism* (mixing of different faiths) and declared he would remove these unholy days from Israel’s calendar.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:11

## Hosea 2:13 (id: 13419)

While the Israelites worshiped Canaanite gods, they forgot the Lord. For Hosea, *to forget* means not knowing the Lord (see [Hosea 8:14](https://ref.ly/Hos8:14); [13:6](https://ref.ly/Hos13:6); [Job 8:12–13](https://ref.ly/Job8:12-Job8:13)). If Israel had truly known the Lord, they would not have worshiped Baal.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:13

## Hosea 2:14–15 (id: 13420)

* The third judgment (see note on [Hosea 2:6–7](https://ref.ly/Hos2:6-Hos2:7)) is a surprise. The Lord, who had innocently suffered from Israel's repeated unfaithfulness, announces he will try to win Israel back. He will lead her into the desert, where he first made a covenant with her, away from the tempting Canaanite religion.
* The Valley of Achor was where Israel first disobeyed after entering the promised land ([Joshua 7:24–26](https://ref.ly/Josh7:24-Josh7:26)). The Lord had the will and power to give Israel a new start after their sin and trouble ([Joshua 8:1–22](https://ref.ly/Josh8:1-Josh8:22)), so Israel would again have a gateway of hope.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:14-15

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Valley of Achor (ID: `place:AchorValley`)
## Hosea 2:16 (id: 13421)

* *that day*: This phrase is important in the prophets. It refers to the coming day of the Lord (see [Joel 1:15](https://ref.ly/Joel1:15); [2:1](https://ref.ly/Joel2:1); [Amos 5:18](https://ref.ly/Amos5:18)), when the Lord will act decisively in human history for his people.
* *you will call Me ‘my Husband,’ and no longer call Me ‘my Master*: On this day, Israel will have a new relationship with God. Israel will no longer call God “master” (Hebrew *ba‘al*), which suggests subservience and was also the name of the main Canaanite fertility god. Instead, she will call the Lord “husband,” implying partnership and companionship ([Genesis 2:23–24](https://ref.ly/Gen2:23-Gen2:24)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:16

## Hosea 2:17 (id: 13422)

To prevent confusion between the Lord and Baal, God would remove Baal's names from the speech of the Israelites.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:17

## Hosea 2:18 (id: 13423)

On that day, the Lord will make a covenant (special agreement) with his creation and people. It will be an agreement of peace and safety.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:18

## Hosea 2:19–20 (id: 13424)

*I will betroth you*: This Hebrew verb, used three times in these verses, can be translated with the word *betroth*. In ancient times, betrothal included all the legal steps of marriage, like the groom paying a bride price. The only things missing were the marriage ceremony and sexual consummation. The Lord promised to betroth Israel to himself forever. 

To ensure this eternal marriage, God provided a bride price with five priceless qualities: 

1. Righteousness
2. Justice
3. Unfailing love
4. Compassion
5. Faithfulness

As a result, the Lord said, Israel will finally know me (compare [Hosea 2:13](https://ref.ly/Hos2:13)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:19-20

## Hosea 2:21–22 (id: 13427)

Unfaithful Israel believed her food and clothing came from her lovers, the Baals ([Hosea 2:5](https://ref.ly/Hos2:5)). However, they will learn that the Lord alone provides all fertility and blessings. He will respond to the sky, the clouds, and the earth, starting the true cycle of fertility for the land. When people recognize the Lord as the only source of life, the name Jezreel will regain its true meaning: God plants!

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:21-22

## Hosea 2:23 (id: 13428)

As God promised ([Hosea 2:1](https://ref.ly/Hos2:1)), on the coming day of the Lord, he will change the names of Gomer’s children. He will show compassion to *No Compassion*. To *Not My People*, he will say, *You are My people*. God's people should respond, *You are my God*.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 2:23

## Hosea 3:1 (id: 13434)

* *Go show love to your wife again, though she*: The Lord commanded Hosea to love his wife again, even though she had strayed. This act served as a testimony that the Lord promised to restore wayward Israel.
* *to offer raisin cakes to idols*: The worship of Asherah, a Canaanite fertility goddess, often included offerings of raisin cakes.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 3:1

## Hosea 3:2 (id: 13435)

*So I bought her*: The Bible does not explain why the prophet had to buy his wife. Many believe that the unfaithful Gomer fell into debt and became a slave. The low price Hosea paid suggests that Gomer was seen as a low\-value slave (see [Exodus 21:32](https://ref.ly/Exod21:32); [Leviticus 27:4](https://ref.ly/Lev27:4)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 3:2

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Homer (ID: `realia:Homer`)
## Hosea 3:3–4 (id: 13436)

Just as Hosea’s wife had to live many days without sexual relations, Israel would be without its key institutions, practices, and objects. These included the king or prince, sacrifices, and sacred pillars or idols, which were central to its life and worship.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 3:3-4

## Hosea 3:5 (id: 13437)

The Lord deprived the Israelites of these things to make them return to him with respect and see him as the only source of goodness.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 3:5

## Hosea 4:1 (id: 13444)

*the LORD has a case against the people of the land*: Hosea delivered a divine accusation against Israel for breaking their agreement with the Lord (see also [Isaiah 3:13](https://ref.ly/Isa3:13); [Micah 6:2](https://ref.ly/Mic6:2)). 

The accusations first highlight sins of omission, the qualities that should define the people of Israel but were missing. In Israel, there is no faithfulness, no kindness, and no knowledge of God. These important words describe the inner trust and devotion from which godly lives should arise.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:1

Hosea shares God's accusations against Israel, warns them of severe consequences for their sins, and ends with a promise of future restoration.

## Hosea 4:2 (id: 13445)

The prophet also accused the Israelites of committing sins. These crimes are all forbidden in the Ten Commandments ([Exodus 20:3–17](https://ref.ly/Exod20:3-Exod20:17); [Deuteronomy 5:7–21](https://ref.ly/Deut5:7-Deut5:21)), which are the basic covenant responsibilities. Since the Israelites did not know the Lord, they failed to follow even the simplest covenant standards.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:2

## Hosea 4:3 (id: 13446)

God's judgment will always come to sinful people. Because the Israelites sinned, their land and nature would suffer.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:3

## Hosea 4:4–5 (id: 13447)

* When accused of a crime, people often blame others. However, God made it clear that Israel’s apostasy was the fault of its religious leaders, priests, and false prophets.
* God’s judgment on the priests and prophets was that they would stumble and fall, both in their work and in their personal lives. (The same Hebrew word is used for both kinds of stumbling.)

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:4-5

## Hosea 4:6 (id: 13448)

* Since the priests refused to know the Lord, the people also did not know their God. As a result, the people were being destroyed.
* A main role of the priests of Israel was to know, practice, and teach the laws of God. Ironically, these guardians of the law had forgotten it.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:6

## Hosea 4:9 (id: 13449)

The people copied the bad behavior of their religious leaders. As a result, God would punish both the priests and the people for their wrong actions.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:9

## Hosea 4:10 (id: 13439)

God's judgment fits the punishment to the crime. The Israelites worshiped Canaanite fertility gods, hoping for abundant crops and herds. However, God's judgment was that they would remain hungry, and their religious practices would bring them no benefits.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:10

## Hosea 4:12 (id: 13440)

*My people consult their wooden idols*: For thousands of years, people have worshiped natural objects like wood and stones, thinking they held spirits and gods ([Jeremiah 2:27](https://ref.ly/Jer2:27)). Biblical religion completely rejects these practices and beliefs ([Exodus 20:4–5](https://ref.ly/Exod20:4-Exod20:5); [Isaiah 44:19](https://ref.ly/Isa44:19)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:12

## Hosea 4:13 (id: 13441)

* Many Canaanite religious rituals took place on mountaintops and hills. On these pagan "high places," sacred trees were often used in fertility worship ([1 Kings 14:23](https://ref.ly/1Kgs14:23); [Jeremiah 2:20](https://ref.ly/Jer2:20)).
* Israelite daughters and brides also engaged in sexual acts during the worship of Baal; their fathers and husbands possibly forced them to participate.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:13

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Styrax (ID: `flora:Styrax`)

* *prostitutes*: The Hebrew word refers to female prostitutes dedicated to serving Baal and Asherah in Baal's temples ([Deuteronomy 23:18](https://ref.ly/Deut23:18)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 4:14

## Hosea 4:15 (id: 13443)

## Hosea 5:1 (id: 13454)

* Hosea criticizes Israel's religious and political leaders. They have led the people into idol worship, like hunters trapping animals.
* The specific sins at Mizpah and Tabor are unknown.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:1

## Hosea 5:2 (id: 13455)

*I will chastise them all*: This refers to God’s judgment. The Hebrew word *musar* means “discipline.” God’s judgment would certainly fall on Israel’s leaders. God did not judge Israel only to punish them, but to correct them so that his people would return to him.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:2

## Hosea 5:3 (id: 13456)

*Israel is defiled*: The word translated as *defiled* often appears in the first five books of the Bible to describe ritual uncleanness. This physical condition prevented a person from worshiping God in the temple ([Leviticus 11–15](https://ref.ly/Lev11:1-Lev15:33)). The prophets used this idea as a metaphor for Israel’s moral uncleanness due to her idolatry ([Isaiah 6:5](https://ref.ly/Isa6:5); [Micah 2:10](https://ref.ly/Mic2:10)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:3

## Hosea 5:4 (id: 13457)

* Israel’s worship of idols was so deeply rooted that the people seemed unable to return to the Lord.
* *the spirit of prostitution is within them*: Israel’s sin was not only an action. It had become part of the nation’s basic character. The people naturally chose what was corrupt instead of what was pure. Only through God’s saving work could Israel be freed from this.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:4

## Hosea 5:6–7 (id: 13458)

People might believe they can earn God's favor with sacrifices. However, Hosea warned them that they would not find him. God had left them to face the consequences of their sins.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:6-7

## Hosea 5:8–9 (id: 13459)

* Hosea warned the Israelites about God's coming judgment. Due to their sins, the Lord had become Israel's enemy and would punish them.
* Gibeah, Ramah, and Beth\-aven (another name for Bethel) are cities in the tribal territory of Benjamin.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:8-9

## Hosea 5:10 (id: 13450)

*those who move boundary stones*: The Lord owned the land and entrusted it to the tribes after the conquest (see [Joshua 13:8–19:51](https://ref.ly/Josh13:8-Josh19:51)). Moving a boundary marker and changing God's allotted boundaries was stealing from God ([Deuteronomy 19:14](https://ref.ly/Deut19:14)). Such an act rightly brought divine punishment ([Deuteronomy 27:17](https://ref.ly/Deut27:17)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:10

## Hosea 5:12 (id: 13451)

*a moth … decay*: Sin can slowly weaken a people at their core, and they may not realize the damage until it is already done.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:12

## Hosea 5:13 (id: 13452)

To prevent complete destruction, the Israelites removed King Pekah and made Hoshea the new king. Hoshea asked the Assyrian king Shalmaneser for peace ([2 Kings 15:29–30](https://ref.ly/2Kgs15:29-2Kgs15:30); [17:3](https://ref.ly/2Kgs17:3)), but these efforts could not save Israel. Assyria would soon destroy them.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:13

## Hosea 5:15 (id: 13453)

Hosea reminded Israel that divine judgment was not just punishment. God's goal was to persuade Israel to admit their guilt and return to the Lord (see [Hosea 2:2](https://ref.ly/Hos2:2), [14](https://ref.ly/Hos2:14)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 5:15

## Hosea 6:1–3 (id: 13460)

* The rains that came in early spring were essential for growing crops.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 6:1-3

Israel's response was not enough. God lamented that their love vanished like morning mist and dew.

## Hosea 6:6 (id: 13463)

In one of Hosea’s key verses, God highlights the most important qualities for the Israelites' covenant lives with him:

1. The Lord wants his people to show love more than he wants sacrifices. Love (the Hebrew word *khesed*, meaning loyal love shown through steady faithfulness) includes being faithful in relationships (see [Hosea 4:1](https://ref.ly/Hos4:1); [6:4](https://ref.ly/Hos6:4)). God is always faithful ([Psalm 136](https://ref.ly/Ps136:1-Ps136:26)), and he expects the same from his people.

## Hosea 14:1–9 (id: 13409)

Destruction was not God's final message to his covenant people. Although judgment must happen, God's healing and restoring grace is stronger than human sin.

## Hosea 14:2–3 (id: 13410)

* Hosea created a prayer of confession to guide his people back to the Lord. God’s prophets felt both God's anger over Israel’s rebellion and the people's suffering (see also [Daniel 9:4–19](https://ref.ly/Dan9:4-Dan9:19)).
* *Take away all our iniquity*: The Israelites should rely on God’s mercy and grace, openly admitting their many sins.
* *that we may present the fruit of our lip**s*: Israel often offered animal sacrifices for sin, but the Lord made it clear that sacrifices were not the answer (see [Hosea 6:6](https://ref.ly/Hos6:6)). Israel should give God true repentance.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 14:2-3

## Hosea 14:3 (id: 13411)

The Israelites must give up their reliance on idols, foreign alliances, and their military strength. Even though they were God's children ([Hosea 11:1](https://ref.ly/Hos11:1)), their rebellion made them orphans. When they sincerely and fully repented, they could find mercy again.

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 14:3

## Hosea 14:4 (id: 13412)

*I will heal their apostasy*: Only God can cure the disease of sin. The Lord heals all our physical and spiritual diseases ([Psalm 103:3](https://ref.ly/Ps103:3)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 14:4

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Out of Free Will (ID: `keyterm:OutOfFreeWill`)
## Hosea 14:5 (id: 13414)

* *take root*: When Israel remained faithful to their covenant with the Lord, they became solid and stable like the majestic cedars in Lebanon ([Psalm 92:12](https://ref.ly/Ps92:12)).

* **Associated Passages:** Hosea 14:5
