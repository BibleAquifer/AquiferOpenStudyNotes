## John 1:1 (id: 18794)

*In the beginning*: Using the same phrase as the beginning of the Old Testament ([Genesis 1:1](https://ref.ly/Gen1:1)), the Gospel of John introduces Jesus Christ as the one through whom God created everything ([John 1:3](https://ref.ly/John1:3)). Through Jesus, God also creates new life in those who believe ([1:12–13](https://ref.ly/John1:12-John1:13)). 

The Gospel of John begins with its central claim: Jesus Christ, the Word (*logos*), not only revealed God but was God. The Greek word *logos* means “word” or “message.” The *logos* is the Word of God, through whom God created everything, made himself known, and became human as Jesus Christ. Later Christian teaching describes the Word as the second person of the Trinity (the one who shares fully in God’s nature with the Father and the Holy Spirit).

In Greek thought, the *logos* was the rational principle that ordered the universe and made life understandable. It explained why the world had meaning and structure.

For Jewish people, the *logos* was the word of the Lord. It expressed God’s wisdom and God’s creative power. God spoke, and things came into being.

By the time of Jesus, people understood the *logos* as coming from God and sharing God’s character and authority (see [Psalm 33:6](https://ref.ly/Ps33:6), [9](https://ref.ly/Ps33:9); [Proverbs 8:22–31](https://ref.ly/Prov8:22-Prov8:31)). John affirmed this understanding by declaring that "the Word \[*logos*] became flesh and made His dwelling among us" ([John 1:14](https://ref.ly/John1:14)).

* **Associated Passages:** John 1:1

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)

## John 1:4 (id: 18813)

* *In Him was life*: Life was God’s original gift to his creatures at creation ([Genesis 1:20–28](https://ref.ly/Gen1:20-Gen1:28); [2:7](https://ref.ly/Gen2:7)). Now the *logos* gives people the possibility of new life, which comes through rebirth from God ([John 1:13](https://ref.ly/John1:13)). * As one of his first creative acts, God brought light into the world ([Genesis 1:3](https://ref.ly/Gen1:3)). In the re\-creation of humankind through Jesus Christ, God offered light and life again. Light is a key theme throughout the Gospel of John.

## John 1:14 (id: 18788)

* *The Word became flesh*: This means the Word became truly human. The idea that the Word became human was shocking to both Greeks and Jews.

    Many Greeks believed that God was completely separate from the ordinary human world. They used the word *sarx* (Greek for “flesh”) to describe human life as weak or earthly. John taught something very different. He said that God himself became human in Jesus Christ (compare [1:1](https://ref.ly/John1:1)).

    Jesus was fully human and fully divine. He was not partly God and partly human. These two truths together form the foundation of Christian teaching about Christ.

* *the Word … made His dwelling among us* (Greek *skēnoō,* “pitched a tent”): The Greek verb *skēnoō* means “to pitch a tent” or “to live in a tent.” This word is related to *skēnē*, the Greek word for the tabernacle. The tabernacle was the tent where the Lord’s glory lived among Israel in the wilderness ([Exodus 25:8–9](https://ref.ly/Exod25:8-Exod25:9)). In Jesus, God’s glory now lived among his people in a new way ([Exodus 40:34–38](https://ref.ly/Exod40:34-Exod40:38); [John 2:11](https://ref.ly/John2:11); [12:23–28](https://ref.ly/John12:23-John12:28), [41](https://ref.ly/John12:41); [17:1–5](https://ref.ly/John17:1-John17:5)).
* Even though the world was dark and hostile, Jesus came to save it. He offered God’s love and faithfulness to the world ([3:15–17](https://ref.ly/John3:15-John3:17)).

* **Associated Passages:** John 1:14

## John 1:15 (id: 18789)

*He was before m*e: In John the Baptist’s culture, older age was respected and honored ([Leviticus 19:32](https://ref.ly/Lev19:32)). Younger people were expected to honor those who were older, not replace them (contrast [Isaiah 3:5](https://ref.ly/Isa3:5)). 

By saying that Jesus existed before him, John the Baptist pointed to the greater honor of Jesus. He explained that Jesus existed before creation itself, not just before the birth of John ([John 1:1–3](https://ref.ly/John1:1-John1:3)).

## John 2:14–17 (id: 19054)

* The Samaritan woman is very different from Nicodemus. She was a woman, not a man. She was a Samaritan, not a Jew. She had a sinful past, not a reputation for righteousness. She was an outsider, not a respected teacher of Israel.

    Nicodemus came to Jesus at night and did not openly respond to the challenges from Jesus ([3:1–21](https://ref.ly/John3:1-John3:21)). The Samaritan woman spoke with Jesus in the daylight. She recognized him as Lord. She remained in the light and showed signs of becoming a disciple (see [1:35–51](https://ref.ly/John1:35-John1:51)).

## John 5:8–9 (id: 19175)

* Jesus healed the sick man immediately and only asked him to obey. To prove that the man was healed, Jesus told him to pick up his mat and walk.
* Jesus performed this miracle on the Sabbath. The Sabbath was a weekly day of rest. The law of Moses said that no work should be done on that day (based on [Genesis 2:2](https://ref.ly/Gen2:2); [Exodus 20:8](https://ref.ly/Exod20:8)). Jewish tradition identified 39 types of work that were not allowed (*Mishnah Shabbat* [7:2](https://ref.ly/John7:2)). One rule said that a person could not carry an object, such as a sleeping mat, from one place to another ([John 5:10](https://ref.ly/John5:10)).

    Jesus told the healed man to pick up his mat and walk. When the man obeyed Jesus, he broke this later Jewish tradition.

## John 11:50–51 (id: 18864)

John explains that Caiaphas did not speak on his own authority. As high priest that year, he spoke prophetically (as one who speaks a message from God). God used his words to point to the saving purpose of the death of Jesus.
