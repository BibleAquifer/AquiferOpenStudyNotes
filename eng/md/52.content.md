## 1 Thessalonians 2:1 (id: 17233)

The Greek word for *visit* often means the process of entering a city. Ancient speakers were famous for their grand entrances into cities. Paul's arrival in Thessalonica should be judged by its successful outcomes, not by its lack of display or ceremony.

* **Associated Passages:** 1 Thessalonians 2:1

## 1 Thessalonians 2:1–3.13 (id: 17222)

The letter begins by discussing the character of Paul and his associates, as well as how the Thessalonians accepted the good news ([2:1–12](https://ref.ly/1Thess2:1-1Thess2:12); [13–16](https://ref.ly/1Thess2:13-1Thess2:16)).

It emphasizes Paul’s genuine care for the church, mentioning: 

* his efforts to visit them again ([2:17–20](https://ref.ly/1Thess2:17-1Thess2:20)), and
* his choice to send Timothy when he could not go himself ([3:1–5](https://ref.ly/1Thess3:1-1Thess3:5)).

It concludes with the apostle Paul’s happiness about Timothy’s positive report and his prayers to return to the church ([3:6–9](https://ref.ly/1Thess3:6-1Thess3:9); [10–13](https://ref.ly/1Thess3:10-1Thess3:13)). This detailed defense addresses criticisms of Paul’s sudden departure and long absence.

## 1 Thessalonians 2:11 (id: 17224)

In the ancient world, fathers had a key role in teaching and modeling moral living to children. Roman fathers could be strict. But the historian Plutarch advised Greek fathers to use praise, reason, encouragement, and good advice to teach children to avoid bad behavior and practice good values. Paul describes himself as this type of father to the Thessalonians.

* **Associated Passages:** 1 Thessalonians 2:11

## 1 Thessalonians 5:15 (id: 17271)

Jesus and the apostles advised against revenge ([Matthew 5:38–48](https://ref.ly/Matt5:38-Matt5:48); [Luke 6:27–36](https://ref.ly/Luke6:27-Luke6:36); [Romans 12:17–21](https://ref.ly/Rom12:17-Rom12:21); [1 Peter 3:9](https://ref.ly/1Pet3:9)). In contrast, the Roman author Seneca believed revenge was necessary to restore lost social honor. Paul urged Christians to do good to everyone, including church members and those outside the Christian community, even those who persecuted them ([1 Thessalonians 2:14](https://ref.ly/1Thess2:14); see [3:12](https://ref.ly/1Thess3:12); [Galatians 6:10](https://ref.ly/Gal6:10)).

* **Associated Passages:** 1 Thessalonians 5:15

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Good (ID: `keyterm:Good`)

## 1 Thessalonians 5:20–21 (id: 17276)

*Do not treat prophecies with contempt:* No one should dismiss prophecy intended to spiritually strengthen the church ([1 Corinthians 14:1–4](https://ref.ly/1Cor14:1-1Cor14:4)). Instead, the Thessalonians should evaluate the prophecies to understand where they come from. Paul says they should make sure any prophecies align with the apostles' teachings ([1 Corinthians 12:10](https://ref.ly/1Cor12:10); [14:29](https://ref.ly/1Cor14:29); [2 Thessalonians 2:2](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:2); [1 John 4:1–3](https://ref.ly/1John4:1-1John4:3)). The church should also assess the character of those who deliver the prophecies ([Matthew 7:15–20](https://ref.ly/Matt7:15-Matt7:20)). 

Early Christian teaching stated, “Not everyone who speaks about spiritual things is a prophet, but only if the person’s behavior is like the Lord” (*Didache*11:8\). Paul encourages the Thessalonians to hold fast to what is good. Specifically, this means to test prophecies to see if they prove true.

* **Associated Passages:** 1 Thessalonians 5:20-21

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Interpret (ID: `keyterm:Interpret`)
