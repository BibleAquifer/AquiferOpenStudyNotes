## Joel 2:1–11 (id: 18741)

Some scholars understand this section as a second description of the locust plague in [chapter 1](https://ref.ly/Joel1:1-Joel1:20). But in [chapter 1](https://ref.ly/Joel1:1-Joel1:20), the plague is described as a past event. In this section, the verb tenses seem to describe a future event.

For this reason, other scholars understand the passage as a warning of another locust plague that has not yet happened.

Still other commentators understand this passage as apocalyptic language (symbolic language about God’s future judgment). In this view, the writer uses the imagery of a locust plague to describe an invading human army on the coming day of the Lord.

* **Associated Passages:** Joel 2:1-11

## Joel 2:28–29 (id: 18760)

*I will pour out My Spirit on all people*: In Israel, the empowering gift of God’s Spirit had previously been given only to select individuals such as: 

* judges ([Judges 3:10](https://ref.ly/Judg3:10); [15:14](https://ref.ly/Judg15:14)),
* priests ([2 Chronicles 24:20](https://ref.ly/2Chr24:20)),
* kings ([1 Samuel 10:10](https://ref.ly/1Sam10:10)), and
* prophets ([Isaiah 61:1](https://ref.ly/Isa61:1)).

A time was coming when the Holy Spirit would be given to everyone among God’s people, regardless of gender, age, or social status. This fulfilled what Moses wanted, that every Israelite might be a prophet ([Numbers 11:29](https://ref.ly/Num11:29)), allowing all to prophesy and see visions. 

In [Acts 2:1–47](https://ref.ly/Acts2:1-Acts2:47), Peter expanded this promise to include people from all over the known world who were gathered in Jerusalem, regardless of their ethnicity ([Acts 2:39](https://ref.ly/Acts2:39); see also [Galatians 3:28](https://ref.ly/Gal3:28)).

* **Associated Passages:** Joel 2:28-29

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Speak as a Prophet (ID: `keyterm:Speak`)

## Joel 2:32 (id: 18765)

* *everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will be saved*: Calling on God's name should not be a final act of desperation. It should be part of a lifelong commitment to worship, serve, and have fellowship with the Lord (see [Genesis 12:8](https://ref.ly/Gen12:8); [Psalm 105:1](https://ref.ly/Ps105:1); [Isaiah 12:4](https://ref.ly/Isa12:4); [Romans 10:13](https://ref.ly/Rom10:13); [12:1–2](https://ref.ly/Rom12:1-Rom12:2)).
* Those whom the Lord has called are his chosen remnant. They are the survivors of judgment who worship him (see [2 Kings 19:31](https://ref.ly/2Kgs19:31); [Ezra 9:8–15](https://ref.ly/Ezra9:8-Ezra9:15); [Isaiah 10:20–22](https://ref.ly/Isa10:20-Isa10:22)).

## Joel 3:2–3 (id: 18779)

* The Lord would gather the armies of the world (literally *all the nations*) in the valley of Jehoshaphat. This valley is not related to King Jehoshaphat of Judah ([1 Kings 22:41](https://ref.ly/1Kgs22:41)). Instead, it means “the valley where the Lord judges.” It is the place where God would decide the fate of the nations (see [Joel 3:14](https://ref.ly/Joel3:14)).
* The sins of the nations included driving God’s covenant people into exile. They also included abusing helpless boys and girls by selling them as slaves (compare [Ezekiel 27:13](https://ref.ly/Ezek27:13); [Revelation 18:13](https://ref.ly/Rev18:13)).

* **Associated Passages:** Joel 3:2-3

## Joel 3:10 (id: 18771)

*Beat your plowshares into swords*: This command deliberately reverses the picture of peace found in [Isaiah 2:4](https://ref.ly/Isa2:4) and [Micah 4:3](https://ref.ly/Mic4:3). Instead of laying down weapons, the nations were told to prepare for war.

Joel ordered them to gather every possible resource for the coming battle. Even those who were weak and not fit for war were told to become soldiers.

## Joel 3:20–21 (id: 18780)

The end of Joel’s prophecy is the opposite of its beginning. In [Joel 1:1–20](https://ref.ly/Joel1:1-Joel1:20), Judah’s future seemed uncertain. Plague and drought, signs of divine judgment, threatened Judah’s survival. Now Joel concludes with the divine promise that Judah and Jerusalem will last through all generations. God's people in every era find security in the hope that his kingdom will rule over heaven and earth (see also [Isaiah 9:6–7](https://ref.ly/Isa9:6-Isa9:7); [Daniel 2:44](https://ref.ly/Dan2:44); [7:13–14](https://ref.ly/Dan7:13-Dan7:14)).
