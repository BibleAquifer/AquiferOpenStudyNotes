## Amos 1:1 (id: 9673)

* *words*: This is the usual way to introduce a prophetic message ([Jeremiah 1:1](https://ref.ly/Jer1:1); see also [Haggai 1:12](https://ref.ly/Hag1:12)). It emphasizes the form and content of the message.
* *sheepherders* (Hebrew *noqed*): This word appears only one other time in the Old Testament, describing the king of Moab as a *sheep breeder* ([2 Kings 3:4](https://ref.ly/2Kgs3:4)). Amos uses a different Hebrew word for his job in [Amos 7:14](https://ref.ly/Amos7:14) (*boqer*, meaning “herdsman”; see Amos Book Introduction, “The Prophet Amos”). Amos was not a professional prophet serving the court or temple.
* Amos received this message through visions, that is, through God’s direct revelation (see [Isaiah 1:1](https://ref.ly/Isa1:1)).
* Amos describes the earthquake during Uzziah's reign ([Zechariah 14:5](https://ref.ly/Zech14:5)) as an act of God's judgment ([Amos 3:14–15](https://ref.ly/Amos3:14-Amos3:15); [6:11](https://ref.ly/Amos6:11), [14](https://ref.ly/Amos6:14); [8:8](https://ref.ly/Amos8:8); [9:1](https://ref.ly/Amos9:1), [9](https://ref.ly/Amos9:9)).
* Uzziah, also known as Azariah, ruled from 792 to 740 BC. He was the most powerful king of Judah after the kingdom split.
* Jeroboam II, who ruled from 793 to 753 BC, was from Jehu's family line of kings. He took advantage of a time when no strong power controlled the region. During this time, he regained land that the Arameans had taken earlier.

* **Associated Passages:** Amos 1:1

## Amos 1:2 (id: 9674)

* This message speaks about judgment on the day of the Lord (the time when God acts to judge and restore).

* Mount Carmel rises near the Mediterranean Sea and usually has plenty of water, even during dry seasons. If the grass on Mount Carmel dried up, it would be a clear sign of a severe and widespread drought.

## Amos 1:3 (id: 9677)

* *For three transgressions of Damascus, even four*: This phrase describes ongoing rebellion against the natural order set by God. The Hebrew phrase does not describe an exact number. Instead, it points to a pattern of repeated wrongdoing.
* *they threshed*: Threshing grain was the process of separating the grain heads from their husks. People did this by pulling wooden sledges with sharp edges over the cut grain ([Isaiah 41:15](https://ref.ly/Isa41:15); see [Micah 4:13](https://ref.ly/Mic4:13)). This image helps explain how harsh and violent the actions of the people of Damascus were.

* **Associated Passages:** Amos 1:3

## Amos 1:3–5 (id: 9676)

Damascus, the capital of Aram, treated the people of Gilead with great cruelty. Gilead was Israel’s land east of the Jordan River.

By the time Ahab died in 853 BC, Damascus had taken Ramoth in Gilead ([1 Kings 22:3](https://ref.ly/1Kgs22:3)). Around 801 BC, Assyria captured Damascus. After this, the city never again had the power it once did.

## Amos 1:4 (id: 9678)

* *I will send fire upon*: In ancient times, invading armies often burned cities after they conquered them (see also [Amos 1:7](https://ref.ly/Amos1:7), [10](https://ref.ly/Amos1:10), [12](https://ref.ly/Amos1:12), [14](https://ref.ly/Amos1:14); [2:2](https://ref.ly/Amos2:2), [5](https://ref.ly/Amos2:5)). The destruction of Damascus would therefore be an act of God’s judgment on the city.
* Hazael ruled as king of Damascus from about 843 to 802 BC. He took the throne by killing the previous king. Ben\-hadad, Hazael’s son, ruled after him from about 802 to 792 BC.

* **Associated Passages:** Amos 1:4

## Amos 1:5 (id: 9679)

* Aven and Beth\-eden were both linked with Aram and Damascus. The name Aven means “evil” or “wickedness.” It refers to the valley between the Lebanon and Anti\-Lebanon mountains, also known as the plain of Damascus. Beth\-eden was a small city\-state north of Aram. In other biblical texts, it is simply called *Eden* ([2 Kings 19:12](https://ref.ly/2Kgs19:12); [Ezekiel 27:23](https://ref.ly/Ezek27:23)).
* The Arameans originally came from Kir ([Amos 9:7](https://ref.ly/Amos9:7)), probably in southern Babylonia. When Damascus fell, Assyria forced the Arameans to return to Kir ([2 Kings 16:9](https://ref.ly/2Kgs16:9)). Just as Egypt symbolized captivity for Israel (see [Deuteronomy 28:68](https://ref.ly/Deut28:68)), Kir became a symbol of captivity for Aram.

## Amos 1:6 (id: 9680)

The Philistine city of Gaza was located on the seacoast at the southwest edge of Palestine. It served as a gateway for trade between Africa and Asia. Its wrongdoing was engaging in slave trading. Philistia had attacked Israel or Judah and sold entire villages into slavery to Edom.

## Amos 2:6 (id: 9694)

Honorable people are called the *righteous*. They lived in right relationship with God and with other people. When this word appears alongside *poor*, it describes the *righteous needy*, people who were both faithful and poor.

The law given through Moses urged those who had enough to help those in need by lending freely ([Deuteronomy 15:7–11](https://ref.ly/Deut15:7-Deut15:11)). As a last option, people who could not repay their debts could become bond servants. They would repay what they owed through their work ([Leviticus 25:39–43](https://ref.ly/Lev25:39-Lev25:43)). 

In Amos’s time, these righteous needy were sold into bond service for a pair of sandals. This phrase is an exaggeration that shows how small the debt was (see [Amos 8:6](https://ref.ly/Amos8:6)). The sandals may have been given as a pledge for the debt or used as a sign to confirm the agreement (see [Ruth 4:7](https://ref.ly/Ruth4:7)).

## Amos 2:7 (id: 9695)

* To *trample* on people means to treat them in a harsh and cruel way (see also [Amos 8:4](https://ref.ly/Amos8:4)).
* The helpless and oppressed suffered under a system that refused to give them the justice the law required ([Exodus 23:6–8](https://ref.ly/Exod23:6-Exod23:8)).
* When a father and son slept with the same woman, it showed how morally broken the people of Israel had become. The law given through Moses clearly forbade this behavior ([Leviticus 18:7–8](https://ref.ly/Lev18:7-Lev18:8), [15](https://ref.ly/Lev18:15); [20:11–12](https://ref.ly/Lev20:11-Lev20:12)).
* *profane my holy name*: By worshiping fertility gods, Israel and nearby nations practiced “sacred prostitution” (see [Hosea 4:10–14](https://ref.ly/Hos4:10-Hos4:14)). They believed sexual relations with a shrine prostitute would ensure good crops and healthy livestock. This verse suggests these acts were done in the name of the Lord. When performed as religious rituals, these corrupt actions treated God’s name as worthless.

* **Associated Passages:** Amos 2:7

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Poor (ID: `keyterm:Poor`); To Dishonor (ID: `keyterm:Dishonor`)

## Amos 2:8 (id: 9696)

* The irony is that those who oppressed the poor openly displayed their sins during religious festivals.
* The law of Moses allowed a lender to take a poor man’s cloak as security for a debt. However, the cloak had to be returned before nightfall, because nights were cold ([Exodus 22:26–27](https://ref.ly/Exod22:26-Exod22:27); [Deuteronomy 24:12–13](https://ref.ly/Deut24:12-Deut24:13)). The law also said that a widow’s clothing must never be taken as security for a debt ([Deuteronomy 24:17](https://ref.ly/Deut24:17)).
