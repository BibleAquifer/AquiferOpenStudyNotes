## 2 Peter 1:1 (id: 14784)

* Simon was one of the most common Jewish names in the first century. Jesus gave him the name Peter ([Matthew 16:17–18](https://ref.ly/Matt16:17-Matt16:18)). Many people in the Greco\-Roman world would use both their given name in their native language and also a Greek name. They did this because Greek was the common language used for trade and communication (see study note on Acts 7:58).
* *a servant … of Jesus Christ*: In the Old Testament, important leaders of God’s people are called servants of the Lord ([Joshua 14:7](https://ref.ly/Josh14:7); [24:29](https://ref.ly/Josh24:29); [2 Kings 10:10](https://ref.ly/2Kgs10:10)). This title emphasizes Peter’s submission to Christ and also suggests that he had an important role in God’s plan.
* *ours*: This probably refers to Jewish Christians. The recipients of this letter were mostly non\-Jewish (Gentile) Christians. In the new covenant, non\-Jewish and Jewish Christians share the same precious faith and are equal as God's people (see [Galatians 3:26–29](https://ref.ly/Gal3:26-Gal3:29)).
* This is one of the few places in the New Testament where Jesus Christ is called God (see also [John 1:1](https://ref.ly/John1:1); [20:28](https://ref.ly/John20:28); [Romans 9:5](https://ref.ly/Rom9:5); [Titus 2:13](https://ref.ly/Titus2:13); [Hebrews 1:8](https://ref.ly/Heb1:8); [1 John 5:20](https://ref.ly/1John5:20)).

## 2 Peter 1:5–7 (id: 14789)

These verses use a stair\-step structure to show how each virtue leads to the next. This pattern is a common literary device called a *sorites*. The order is not the main point. For example, Peter is not saying that moral excellence must come before knowledge. But he does show that love is the highest and most important virtue in the list ([1:7](https://ref.ly/2Pet1:7)).

* **Associated Passages:** 2 Peter 1:5-7

## 2 Peter 1:12–15 (id: 14779)

Peter was writing at the end of his life, and 2 Peter is similar to works that belong to a popular Jewish genre called the *testament*. In this genre, an older and respected leader gives final instructions to his children or to the people under his care while he is near death (see [Deuteronomy 31–33](https://ref.ly/Deut31:1-Deut33:29); [Joshua 24](https://ref.ly/Josh24:1-Josh24:33)). This genre is especially well known from a Jewish work called *The Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs.*

## 2 Peter 1:19 (id: 14783)

* Here, *day* refers to the day of the Lord. This is a time when God acts in history to save his people and judge his enemies (see [Isaiah 13:4–12](https://ref.ly/Isa13:4-Isa13:12); [Joel 2:1–32](https://ref.ly/Joel2:1-Joel2:32); [Zephaniah 2:1–3](https://ref.ly/Zeph2:1-Zeph2:3)). Christ's first coming began the day of the Lord’s salvation for his people. A future *day* of the Lord’s judgment on his enemies will occur as well.
* *the morning star*: The planet Venus, called the “*morning star*,” often appears above the eastern horizon just before dawn. Peter reverses the normal order of nature. He explains that the dawning of the day of the Lord will lead to the Morning Star, Christ, shining in the hearts of believers.

## 2 Peter 2:1 (id: 14800)

The false teachers already present in the church were the fulfillment of that prophecy.
* *denying the Master who bought them*: The false teachers might have openly rejected Christ. But it is more likely that their bad behavior was a denial of Christ (compare [Titus 1:16](https://ref.ly/Titus1:16)).
