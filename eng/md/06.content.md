## Joshua 1:1–9 (id: 19311)

God confirmed Joshua as Israel's leader and the man to succeed Moses. God promised Abraham that those descended from him would possess the land of Canaan ([Genesis 12:7](https://ref.ly/Gen12:7); [15:7](https://ref.ly/Gen15:7); [17:8](https://ref.ly/Gen17:8)). God repeated this promise to Moses ([Exodus 3:6–8](https://ref.ly/Exod3:6-Exod3:8); [13:3–5](https://ref.ly/Exod13:3-Exod13:5); [Deuteronomy 1:6–8](https://ref.ly/Deut1:6-Deut1:8); [6:10–19](https://ref.ly/Deut6:10-Deut6:19)). Now, God was ready to fulfill this promise through Joshua. 

Joshua led the army of Israel's first people who settled in the land God promised to give Israel. Joshua had victories against two Canaanite groups. First, he won battles in the south and then in the north. This allowed access to the hill country for settlement by the Israelites.

* **Associated Passages:** Joshua 1:1-9

## Joshua 1:6–9 (id: 19322)

For emphasis, God told Joshua three times that he would need to be strong and courageous in order to be successful against the Canaanites. There were a lot more Canaanites than Israelites at the time.

The people also needed to obey all the instructions Moses had given them so they could: 

* settle the land,
* establish farms with successful production, and
* build homes, villages, and cities.

The land belongs to God, as does the whole earth ([Psalm 24:1](https://ref.ly/Ps24:1)). God brought Israel into Canaan as tenants, not as owners ([Leviticus 25:23](https://ref.ly/Lev25:23)).

## Joshua 1:8 (id: 19324)

* This *Book of the Law* (Hebrew *torah,* meaning "instruction") probably refers to the book of Deuteronomy (see [Deuteronomy 4:44](https://ref.ly/Deut4:44); [29:21](https://ref.ly/Deut29:21); [30:10](https://ref.ly/Deut30:10); [31:9–12](https://ref.ly/Deut31:9-Deut31:12)). It includes Israel’s foundational law codes and God’s moral and spiritual instruction.
* God commanded Joshua to think about and speak God’s instruction day and night. This command means that Joshua was to focus on it continually (compare [Deuteronomy 6:6–7](https://ref.ly/Deut6:6-Deut6:7); [17:18–20](https://ref.ly/Deut17:18-Deut17:20)).

## Joshua 24:1–27 (id: 19527)

In both its form and its content, this covenant statement is similar to an ancient Near Eastern suzerain–vassal treaty (a treaty between a powerful ruler and a weaker people). The suzerain protects them and provides benefits, and in return, the weaker people (the vassal) owe loyalty and obedience.

The covenant begins with a preamble, which identifies the speaker ([24:2](https://ref.ly/Josh24:2)). It then includes a historical prologue. This section recounts the suzerain’s gracious acts, describing what God had done for the people in the past ([24:3–13](https://ref.ly/Josh24:3-Josh24:13)).

Next, the covenant lists the stipulations, or required obligations, that the people must follow ([24:14–15](https://ref.ly/Josh24:14-Josh24:15)). It also warns of curses and promises blessings, depending on whether the people obey or disobey ([24:19–20](https://ref.ly/Josh24:19-Josh24:20)).

Finally, the passage notes where the covenant text was recorded so it could be read again in the future (implied in [24:26](https://ref.ly/Josh24:26)). It also names witnesses who confirmed the covenant agreement ([24:22](https://ref.ly/Josh24:22), [27](https://ref.ly/Josh24:27)). See also study note on Exodus 20:1–23:33.
