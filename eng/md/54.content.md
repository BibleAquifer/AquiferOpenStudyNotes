## 1 Timothy 1:4 (id: 17305)

* *myths and endless genealogies … speculation*: Paul might be dismissing their teachings as not significant, or he might rejecting their interpretations and their justification of bad behavior ([1:7](https://ref.ly/1Tim1:7); see also [2 Timothy 4:3–4](https://ref.ly/2Tim4:3-2Tim4:4); [Titus 1:14](https://ref.ly/Titus1:14); [3:9](https://ref.ly/Titus3:9); [2 Peter 1:16](https://ref.ly/2Pet1:16); [1 Timothy 1:8–11](https://ref.ly/1Tim1:8-1Tim1:11); [4:1–2](https://ref.ly/1Tim4:1-1Tim4:2), [7](https://ref.ly/1Tim4:7); see also [Titus 1:15–16](https://ref.ly/Titus1:15-Titus1:16)).
* *endless genealogies*: In Judaism, a genealogy (list of ancestors) established a person's spiritual heritage. The false teachers might have focused too much on this. They probably also used Old Testament genealogies in imaginary interpretations of the law ([Genesis 2–4](https://ref.ly/Gen2:1-Gen4:26); [5:1–32](https://ref.ly/Gen5:1-Gen5:32)).
* *rather than the stewardship of God’s work, which is by faith*: The Greek uses an important word (“stewardship,” “household management”) connected to managing a household (see [1 Timothy 3:15](https://ref.ly/1Tim3:15)). This phrase can also imply other meanings, including faithfulness to God's household management or faithfulness in managing God's household.

* **Associated Passages:** 1 Timothy 1:4

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`); Believe (ID: `keyterm:Believe`)

## 1 Timothy 1:10 (id: 17293)

* This list shows the main ideas of the false teachers' teachings and their spiritual condition. It also demonstrates how superior the good news is over the law (see [Matthew 5:20](https://ref.ly/Matt5:20); [Galatians 5:22–23](https://ref.ly/Gal5:22-Gal5:23)).
* *who is averse to sound teaching*: See also [1 Timothy 6:3–4](https://ref.ly/1Tim6:3-1Tim6:4); [2 Timothy 1:13](https://ref.ly/2Tim1:13); [4:3](https://ref.ly/2Tim4:3); [Titus 1:9](https://ref.ly/Titus1:9), [13](https://ref.ly/Titus1:13); [2:1](https://ref.ly/Titus2:1), [8](https://ref.ly/Titus2:8). This addition to the list is both a final summary and a strong statement. The good news about Jesus now defines what makes a person right with God more effectively than the law. But the law still addresses anything that opposes the gospel. The false teachers focused on the law, emphasizing their rejection of Paul's teaching.

## 1 Timothy 1:15 (id: 17296)

*This is a trustworthy saying*: This phrase appears only in the letters to Timothy and Titus ([1 Timothy 3:1](https://ref.ly/1Tim3:1); [4:8–9](https://ref.ly/1Tim4:8-1Tim4:9); [2 Timothy 2:11–13](https://ref.ly/2Tim2:11-2Tim2:13); [Titus 3:4–8](https://ref.ly/Titus3:4-Titus3:8)). These sayings are probably quotes from tradition that Paul has adapted for the current situation. The saying emphasizes the mercy shown to Paul, but it also offers correction to the false teachers. The saying emphasizes Christ's arrival in history to save sinners. 

This saying was probably being suppressed in some way by the false teachers, who focused on:

* law ([1 Timothy 1:7](https://ref.ly/1Tim1:7)),

* special knowledge ([6:20](https://ref.ly/1Tim6:20); see also [Titus 1:16](https://ref.ly/Titus1:16)), and
* rules like those in [1 Timothy 4:3](https://ref.ly/1Tim4:3) (see also [Colossians 2:16–23](https://ref.ly/Col2:16-Col2:23)).

## 1 Timothy 2:2 (id: 17319)

## 1 Timothy 2:5–6 (id: 17320)

Brief teachings like this one appear throughout the letters to Timothy and Titus (see also [1 Timothy 3:16](https://ref.ly/1Tim3:16); [2 Timothy 1:9–10](https://ref.ly/2Tim1:9-2Tim1:10); [2:8](https://ref.ly/2Tim2:8), [11–13](https://ref.ly/2Tim2:11-2Tim2:13); [Titus 3:4–7](https://ref.ly/Titus3:4-Titus3:7)). They might have adapted these teachings from creeds, hymns, or prayers familiar to the churches. The teachings probably relate to Paul's trouble with false teachers. The false teachers' teachings weakened the universal message of the good news about Jesus and the mission to non\-Jews (gentiles). We should use our resources for enjoyment and to share Christ’s love with others (see [1 Timothy 4:3–5](https://ref.ly/1Tim4:3-1Tim4:5); [Psalm 145:16](https://ref.ly/Ps145:16); [Acts 14:17](https://ref.ly/Acts14:17); see [Luke 12:16–34](https://ref.ly/Luke12:16-Luke12:34); [2 Corinthians 8:1–9:15](https://ref.ly/2Cor8:1-2Cor9:15)).

## 1 Timothy 6:17–19 (id: 17370)

According to [6:11–16](https://ref.ly/1Tim6:11-1Tim6:16), it is unwise to value the temporary wealth of this age ([James 5:3](https://ref.ly/Jas5:3)). It is wiser to gather treasure for the future age by trusting in God, doing good deeds, and showing generosity and love to others (see [1 Timothy 1:5](https://ref.ly/1Tim1:5)).

## 1 Timothy 6:20 (id: 17375)

*what has been entrusted to you*(literally *the deposit*): The good news about Jesus is a deposit given to the church and its leaders to keep safe ([1:15](https://ref.ly/1Tim1:15); [2:5–6](https://ref.ly/1Tim2:5-1Tim2:6); [3:16](https://ref.ly/1Tim3:16)). It includes an entire way of life ([2 Timothy 1:13–14](https://ref.ly/2Tim1:13-2Tim1:14); [3:10–11](https://ref.ly/2Tim3:10-2Tim3:11)). It strengthens and cleanses the church when confronted with false teachings in challenging times ([2 Timothy 3:1](https://ref.ly/2Tim3:1)). The deposit is part of the church's tradition (see [2 Timothy 2:2](https://ref.ly/2Tim2:2); [1 Corinthians 11:23–25](https://ref.ly/1Cor11:23-1Cor11:25); [15:1–8](https://ref.ly/1Cor15:1-1Cor15:8); [2 Thessalonians 2:15](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:15); [3:6](https://ref.ly/2Thess3:6)). It needs creative and true interpretation to address changing situations and problems (see [2 Timothy 1:12–14](https://ref.ly/2Tim1:12-2Tim1:14)).

## 1 Timothy 6:21 (id: 17376)

* The letter ends suddenly. There was no need for mission directions (see [Titus 3:12–14](https://ref.ly/Titus3:12-Titus3:14)). Greetings were either not necessary or not appropriate (see [Titus 3:15](https://ref.ly/Titus3:15); [Romans 16:3–16](https://ref.ly/Rom16:3-Rom16:16)).
* *you all*: Paul sent the letter to Timothy. But Paul meant it to be read to the entire church (see 1 Timothy Book Introduction, “Literary Genre”).
