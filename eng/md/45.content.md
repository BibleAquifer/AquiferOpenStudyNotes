## Romans 1:1 (id: 25536)

* *a servant of Christ Jesus*: The word *servant* describes key Old Testament leaders of God's people, such as:

    + Moses ([2 Kings 18:12](https://ref.ly/2Kgs18:12)),
        + Joshua ([Joshua 24:29](https://ref.ly/Josh24:29)),
        + Elijah ([2 Kings 10:10](https://ref.ly/2Kgs10:10)), and
        + David ([2 Samuel 7:8](https://ref.ly/2Sam7:8)).
        This title emphasizes the apostle Paul's total submission to Christ as Lord.

* *set apart*: Paul may be saying that God set him apart for his mission before he was born. This idea is similar to the call of the prophet Jeremiah ([Jeremiah 1:5](https://ref.ly/Jer1:5)). Paul may also be thinking about God’s call during his conversion on the road to Damascus, when God chose him for a special task ([Acts 9:15–16](https://ref.ly/Acts9:15-Acts9:16); compare [Acts 13:2](https://ref.ly/Acts13:2)). God called Paul to preach the good news about Jesus to Jews and especially to Gentiles (non\-Jews).
* The good news, also called "the gospel," appears many times at the beginning of this letter ([Romans 1:1](https://ref.ly/Rom1:1), [9](https://ref.ly/Rom1:9), [15](https://ref.ly/Rom1:15), [16](https://ref.ly/Rom1:16)). Paul takes the word gospel from the Old Testament. There, the Hebrew word refers to news of victory. It describes the victory God wins for his people and the hope he brings to them ([Isaiah 40:9](https://ref.ly/Isa40:9); [41:27](https://ref.ly/Isa41:27); [60:6](https://ref.ly/Isa60:6); [61:1](https://ref.ly/Isa61:1); [Nahum 1:15](https://ref.ly/Nah1:15); see [Joel 2:32](https://ref.ly/Joel2:32)).

* **Associated Passages:** Romans 1:1

## Romans 1:3 (id: 25545)

* *According to the flesh*: Paul often uses the word *flesh* (Greek *sarx*) to speak about bodily life in this world (for example, [4:1](https://ref.ly/Rom4:1); [8:3](https://ref.ly/Rom8:3)).
* Paul mentions King David’s family because God promised that a person descended from David would be the Messiah (God's chosen one) and would have an eternal kingdom ([2 Samuel 7:13–16](https://ref.ly/2Sam7:13-2Sam7:16); see [Isaiah 9:7](https://ref.ly/Isa9:7); [Jeremiah 33:15](https://ref.ly/Jer33:15)). Jesus was born into David’s family line ([Matthew 1:6](https://ref.ly/Matt1:6); [Luke 1:27](https://ref.ly/Luke1:27), [32](https://ref.ly/Luke1:32)). This shows that he met God’s requirement to fulfill this promise.

## Romans 1:5 (id: 25547)

* *received …* *apostleship*: The apostle Paul might be referring to both himself and the other apostles, or he might be using "we" to only refer to himself.
* *we received grace and apostleship*: Paul always makes it evident that his unique authority is a gift from God (see also [15:15–16](https://ref.ly/Rom15:15-Rom15:16)).
* *to the obedience that comes from faith*: This summarizes Paul’s goal in preaching to *Gentiles* (non\-Jews). It frames the book of Romans, as he repeats the idea in different words at the end of the letter ([16:26](https://ref.ly/Rom16:26)). Paul wanted Gentiles to trust in Jesus Christ. He emphasized that trusting in Jesus Christ as the Lord means committing to obey him. Faith and obeying are not the same, but one does not happen without the other.

## Romans 1:13 (id: 25530)

* *brothers*: The Greek word *adelphoi* describes people who are in a family relationship. Paul and other New Testament writers use this word to show that Christians belong to one family because they are united in Christ. The word refers to both male and female Christians.
* *but have been prevented from visiting until now*: Paul wrote this letter while in Corinth near the end of his third missionary journey (see [Acts 20:2–4](https://ref.ly/Acts20:2-Acts20:4); compare [Romans 16:21–23](https://ref.ly/Rom16:21-Rom16:23)). Paul had been busy planting and supporting churches in the eastern Mediterranean. Before visiting the Roman Christians, he needed to return to Jerusalem to deliver money collected from the Gentile (non\-Jewish) churches for the poor Jewish Christians ([15:23–29](https://ref.ly/Rom15:23-Rom15:29)).

* **Associated Passages:** Romans 1:13

## Romans 1:14 (id: 25531)

*to Greeks and non\-Greeks*: The Greeks were proud of being educated and cultured. They often thought people from other cultures were inferior. They mocked the way foreigners spoke Greek, saying it sounded like “bar bar,” a meaningless sound. From this mockery comes the word *barbarian* (a person seen as uncivilized). Paul refers to this cultural divide to show that he wanted to preach the good news about Jesus to every kind of person.

* **Associated Passages:** Romans 1:14

## Romans 1:17 (id: 25533)

* *the righteousness of God*: This important phrase appears eight times in Romans (see also [3:5](https://ref.ly/Rom3:5), [21](https://ref.ly/Rom3:21), [22](https://ref.ly/Rom3:22), [25](https://ref.ly/Rom3:25), [26](https://ref.ly/Rom3:26); [10:3](https://ref.ly/Rom10:3); the only other occurrence in Paul's writings is [2 Corinthians 5:21](https://ref.ly/2Cor5:21)). In the Old Testament, God’s righteousness refers to who God is (holy and faithful) and to what God does when he rescues his people and makes things right (see especially [Isaiah 46:13](https://ref.ly/Isa46:13); [51:5–8](https://ref.ly/Isa51:5-Isa51:8)). Paul uses the second meaning in this verse. The gospel (the good news about Jesus) has the power to save because it fulfills God's promise to defend his people.
* *"The righteous will live by faith"*: The prophet Habakkuk struggled to understand how God could use nations that worshiped false gods to judge his own people, Israel. God reminded Habakkuk that his true people were the righteous, who needed to live by faith ([Habakkuk 2:4](https://ref.ly/Hab2:4)). In [Romans 1–4](https://ref.ly/Rom1:1-Rom4:25), Paul often insists that only by faith can humans become right with God.

* **Associated Passages:** Romans 1:17

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Believe (ID: `keyterm:Believe`); Be (Or Show Oneself) Just (ID: `keyterm:Just`); Life (ID: `keyterm:Life`); LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)

## Romans 1:18 (id: 25535)

*The wrath of God*: God’s wrath (or anger) is not a sudden emotional reaction. It is the holy God’s necessary response to human sin. Because God is just and good, God must respond to what destroys life and faithfulness.

The Old Testament often describes God’s wrath in specific historical events, such as Israel’s rebellion or injustice among the people ([Exodus 32:10–12](https://ref.ly/Exod32:10-Exod32:12); [Numbers 11:1](https://ref.ly/Num11:1); [Jeremiah 21:3–7](https://ref.ly/Jer21:3-Jer21:7)). It also looks ahead to a future time when God will judge human sin in a final and decisive way.

Paul usually speaks of God’s wrath as something that will happen at the end of history ([Romans 2:5](https://ref.ly/Rom2:5), [8](https://ref.ly/Rom2:8); [5:9](https://ref.ly/Rom5:9); [Colossians 3:6](https://ref.ly/Col3:6); [1 Thessalonians 1:10](https://ref.ly/1Thess1:10)). However, when Paul uses the present tense and says that God’s wrath “is revealed,” he points to God’s actions in history as well. These passages show that God has already responded to sin throughout human history, not only at the end of time.

* **Associated Passages:** Romans 1:18

* **Associated ACAI Concepts:** Unjust Deed (ID: `keyterm:UnjustDeed`); Truth (ID: `keyterm:Truth`); Sky (ID: `keyterm:Heaven`); LORD (ID: `deity:Lord`)

Because of this, both groups are in the same situation before God. No one can gain God’s favor by their own actions or obedience ([3:9–20](https://ref.ly/Rom3:9-Rom3:20)). All people need God’s saving action, not their own efforts.

## Romans 1:21 (id: 25537)

In Scripture, to know God usually means to have a close and saving relationship with him (see [2 Corinthians 5:16](https://ref.ly/2Cor5:16); [Galatians 4:9](https://ref.ly/Gal4:9); [Philippians 3:8](https://ref.ly/Phil3:8), [10](https://ref.ly/Phil3:10)). Here, however, *they knew God* means that people knew about God. All people have some understanding of God through creation, but they do not do what is right based on that knowledge. Instead of learning more about God, they worship gods they create themselves.

## Romans 1:26 (id: 25539)

*Even their women exchanged natural relations for unnatural ones*: In this context, *the natural way* refers to the nature of the world as God made it.

As in the Old Testament, Paul refers to same\-sex sexual behavior as one example of how people have turned away from honoring the true God (see [Genesis 19:1–28](https://ref.ly/Gen19:1-Gen19:28); [Leviticus 18:22](https://ref.ly/Lev18:22); [Leviticus 20:13](https://ref.ly/Lev20:13); [Deuteronomy 23:17–18](https://ref.ly/Deut23:17-Deut23:18)).

God created human beings as male and female, and engaging in same\-sex sexual activity is a violation of God’s creative intention.

## Romans 2:4 (id: 25667)

*not realizing that God’s kindness leads you to repentance**?*: Jewish writings describe a common attitude of false security about judgment (for example, *Wisdom of Solomon* 12–15; compare [Jeremiah 7:1–5](https://ref.ly/Jer7:1-Jer7:5); [Amos 5:18–27](https://ref.ly/Amos5:18-Amos5:27)). They thought their sins would not be punished in the same way as the sins of Gentiles. Paul makes clear that God’s grace was meant to turn Jews away from sin, not to approve of a sinful way of life.

## Romans 2:7 (id: 25669)

*To those … doing good … He will give eternal life*: Paul explains elsewhere that no one can receive eternal life except as God's gift through faith ([3:20](https://ref.ly/Rom3:20), [28](https://ref.ly/Rom3:28); [4:1–8](https://ref.ly/Rom4:1-Rom4:8)). Here, Paul may be speaking about Christians whose good actions come from faith and will be considered in God’s judgment. Or he may be pointing to God’s perfect standard of holiness, which shows that only complete perfection could bring acceptance before God. As Paul’s argument continues, he shows that no one is able to meet this standard.

## Romans 2:12 (id: 25656)

* *Perish*: This common New Testament word describes the final outcome of the wicked after judgment (see also [9:22](https://ref.ly/Rom9:22); [14:15](https://ref.ly/Rom14:15); [1 Corinthians 1:18](https://ref.ly/1Cor1:18); [15:18](https://ref.ly/1Cor15:18); [2 Corinthians 2:15](https://ref.ly/2Cor2:15); [4:3](https://ref.ly/2Cor4:3); [Philippians 1:28](https://ref.ly/Phil1:28); [3:19](https://ref.ly/Phil3:19); [2 Thessalonians 2:10](https://ref.ly/2Thess2:10); [1 Timothy 6:9](https://ref.ly/1Tim6:9)). It refers to complete ruin and loss under God’s judgment.
* *all … under the law*: The Jews received the law of Moses, while the Gentiles (non\-Jewish people) never had God’s written law. In the New Testament period, Jews saw having the law as a mark of God's favor towards them and even as a guarantee of salvation.

## Romans 2:14–15 (id: 25658)

*Gentiles, who do not have the law, do by nature what the law requires*: This fits with the idea that the law is written on their hearts ([2:15](https://ref.ly/Rom2:15)), which points to the promise of the new covenant ([Jeremiah 31:31–34](https://ref.ly/Jer31:31-Jer31:34)).

However, in the later part of the Old Testament and in Jewish teaching, a new idea developed. This idea is often called a “remnant,” meaning a smaller group within Israel who remain faithful to God (see [Isaiah 11:10–16](https://ref.ly/Isa11:10-Isa11:16)). 

In the New Testament, *Israel* can sometimes have a broader meaning. On at least one occasion, it refers to all people who belong to God in a spiritual sense, including both Jews and Gentiles ([Galatians 6:16](https://ref.ly/Gal6:16)). 

Paul teaches that there is now an “Israel within Israel.” This means that not all who belong to Israel by birth truly belong to God’s people. God’s true people are those who believe, whether they are Jews or Gentiles (compare [Romans 11:16–17](https://ref.ly/Rom11:16-Rom11:17); [Galatians 6:16](https://ref.ly/Gal6:16)).

## Romans 15:33 (id: 25630)

Some manuscripts do not include the word *Amen*. One very early manuscript places the doxology (a short hymn of praise, [16:25–27](https://ref.ly/Rom16:25-Rom16:27)) here. The best early manuscripts place the doxology at the end of chapter [16](https://ref.ly/Rom16:1-Rom16:27). Most likely, all of chapter [16](https://ref.ly/Rom16:1-Rom16:27) was part of Paul’s original letter to the Romans. See also the study note on 16:1–16.

## Romans 16:25 (id: 25648)

*the mystery*: For Paul, a mystery is a truth about God and his plan that was not clearly known in the time of the Old Testament. God has now made this plan known in the time of the New Testament. ## Romans 16:25–27 (id: 25647)

* This doxology (a short hymn of praise) provides a fitting conclusion to Paul’s letter and its message. It brings together many of the same themes found at the beginning of the letter ([1:1–15](https://ref.ly/Rom1:1-Rom1:15)).
* The doxology is missing from two later manuscripts and appears in different places in some others, such as after [14:23](https://ref.ly/Rom14:23) or after [15:33](https://ref.ly/Rom15:33). Because of this, some have suggested that these verses were added later. However, most manuscripts place the doxology at the end of the letter. Its language and themes also match the rest of Romans. For these reasons, Paul most likely wrote it himself as the letter’s conclusion.

* **Associated Passages:** Romans 16:25-27
